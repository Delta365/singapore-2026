# Singapore public transport mechanics for a short-stay visitor, November 2026

Scope note: researched 24 to 25 October 2026 for a couple travelling 2 to 9 November 2026 (8 days), based at Hillview on the Downtown Line, travelling mostly by public transport. All prices in Singapore dollars.

**Timing point that governs everything below.** Two fare revisions are in play and only one of them applies to this trip.

- The fares in force in November 2026 are those set by the 2025 fare review, effective **27 December 2025**. Confirmed and in effect.
- A further increase of 7.0 percent was announced in the 2026 fare review and takes effect **26 December 2026**, which is after the trip. Announced, not yet in effect. It does not affect November 2026 travel.

## Ways to pay for MRT and buses as a tourist

### Takeaway
Tapping a foreign contactless Visa, Mastercard or American Express card (or the same card in Apple Pay or Google Pay) is the simplest option and needs no purchase, registration or top-up. The single catch is a S$0.60 Foreign Bank Card Admin Fee charged for each day the card is used, plus whatever foreign transaction fee the home bank adds. For an 8-day stay that fee totals about S$4.80 per person, which is less than the non-refundable S$5 card cost of a physical EZ-Link card.

### Cited Findings

**SimplyGo with contactless bank cards (the default recommendation)**
- Accepted cards are "American Express, Mastercard, NETS and Visa bank cards with the EMVCo Contactless Indicator, including foreign-issued cards". Maestro and Cirrus debit cards and NETSPay are not accepted — [SimplyGo FAQ: contactless bank cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/faqs/cards-and-charms/simplygo/contactless-bank-cards/)
- "A SGD0.60 Foreign Bank Card Admin Fee per day will apply for foreign-issued Mastercard or Visa debit/credit cards." The fee is charged per day of use, not per journey, and issuing banks may impose additional fees — [SimplyGo FAQ: contactless bank cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/faqs/cards-and-charms/simplygo/contactless-bank-cards/)
- "You do not need to sign up for a SimplyGo account to use your contactless bank card for transit." Registration is optional and only adds travel history and trip notifications — [SimplyGo FAQ: contactless bank cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/faqs/cards-and-charms/simplygo/contactless-bank-cards/)
- Settlement behaviour differs by network. Mastercard fares "will be accumulated for up to five days" or until S$15 of transit fares is reached, whichever comes first. Visa, American Express and NETS accumulate daily. The accumulated amount is posted to the bank after three days — [SimplyGo FAQ: contactless bank cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/faqs/cards-and-charms/simplygo/contactless-bank-cards/)
- American Express contactless cards work with Apple Pay, Google Pay and Samsung Pay. The FAQ warns that Google Pay users may hit problems in transit due to an expired certificate, and suggests removing and re-adding the card — [SimplyGo FAQ: contactless bank cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/faqs/cards-and-charms/simplygo/contactless-bank-cards/)
- Critical operational warning: tap in and out with the same card and the same device. "Using a mix of card, mobile wallet or different mobile device when tapping in and out may result in multiple charges," even when the same card sits in several wallets — [SimplyGo FAQ: contactless bank cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/faqs/cards-and-charms/simplygo/contactless-bank-cards/)
- Changi Airport's own transport page confirms contactless Mastercard, Visa and NETS bank cards and mobile wallets are accepted for public transport, and states that "foreign-issued bank cards incur admin fees" — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)
- The SimplyGo web Portal was discontinued on 8 January 2026. Travel card and transit services now run through the SimplyGo app — [SimplyGo (TransitLink)](https://simplygo.transitlink.com.sg/)

**SimplyGo EZ-Link card (stored value)**
- S$10 at SimplyGo Ticket Offices and Ticketing Service Centres, made up of S$5 stored value and S$5 card cost. The card cost is non-refundable — [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- Also sold at convenience stores, vending machines, official e-commerce platforms and selected retail outlets, where "price varies" — [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- A minimum S$5 balance is required before first use. Maximum stored value is S$500 — [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- Top-up minimums are S$2 by cash at selected Top-Up Kiosks (exact amount, no change given) or S$10 by NETS, credit or debit card — [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- **Important for visitors:** "Credit/debit cards issued outside Singapore are not accepted" at Assisted Service Kiosks, Top-Up Kiosks or SimplyGo Kiosks. A foreign card therefore cannot be used to top up at a machine; top-ups must be made with cash or at a staffed Ticket Office — [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- Remaining travel value can be refunded at any SimplyGo Ticket Office. The S$5 card cost is not refunded — [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- At Changi, stored-value EZ-Link cards are sold at Changi Recommends stores, the Passenger Service Centre, and the TransitLink Ticket Office at Changi Airport Station — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)

**Singapore Tourist Pass**
- See the dedicated section below for prices and break-even.

**NETS Prepaid card**
- SimplyGo lists NETS Prepaid as a travel card but states it "does not support top-up, refund, or replacement of NETS Prepaid cards" and refers users to NETS — [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- A third-party travel guide puts the card at roughly S$10 total, comprising a non-refundable S$5 card fee plus S$5 stored value, and lists sales points including 7-Eleven, Mustafa, TransitLink Ticket Offices, the NETS Customer Service Centre, Lazada and Shopee — [Trip.com NETS guide](https://sg.trip.com/guide/payments/nets-flashpay.html) (third-party, price not confirmed by NETS or LTA)
- The same guide states NETS FlashPay stopped being accepted for public transport from 1 June 2024. Do not buy or rely on an old FlashPay card — [Trip.com NETS guide](https://sg.trip.com/guide/payments/nets-flashpay.html) (third-party, not confirmed against an LTA source in this research)

**Historical context on the card system**
- LTA reversed a plan to retire the card-based stored-value system in January 2024 and extended the lifespan of the older CBT system to run in parallel with SimplyGo "until at least 2030" — [LandTransportGuru: SimplyGo ABT system](https://landtransportguru.net/simplygo-abt-system/) (enthusiast site reporting LTA announcements)

### Inferences
- Tapping a foreign contactless card is the simplest option for this trip. There is nothing to buy, nothing to queue for on arrival, no unused value stranded on a card at the end, and no need to find a Ticket Office to top up.
- The per-day structure of the S$0.60 admin fee means the cost is fixed regardless of how much the couple travels. Over 8 days it is S$4.80 per person, or S$9.60 for the couple. That is cheaper than two EZ-Link cards (S$10 of non-refundable card cost between them) before any consideration of top-up friction.
- The couple should each use a different physical card or device and keep to it for the whole trip. Two people cannot tap in on one card, and switching between a plastic card and a phone mid-journey risks a double charge.
- Because foreign cards cannot top up at kiosks, an EZ-Link card is a worse choice for a visitor than it looks. Running low on value means carrying cash or finding a staffed office.
- A home bank that charges a percentage foreign transaction fee will apply it to the batched settlement amounts rather than to each tap, so the overhead is small in absolute terms. A card with no foreign transaction fee removes it entirely.

### Gaps
- No official statement found on whether the S$0.60 admin fee is charged once per card per day or once per card per operator per day. Assume once per card per day.
- No official confirmation found of the NETS Prepaid card price from NETS or LTA.
- No official LTA or SimplyGo page found confirming the 1 June 2024 withdrawal of NETS FlashPay from transit. The claim comes from a third-party guide only.

## Singapore Tourist Pass cost and break-even

### Takeaway
Confirmed prices are S$17 for 1 day, S$24 for 2 days and S$29 for 3 days. Against the current adult card fare ceiling of S$2.57 per journey, the 1-day pass needs roughly 7 journeys in a day to pay for itself and the 3-day pass needs roughly 4 journeys a day. For a couple making 3 to 4 MRT trips a day from Hillview, pay-as-you-go on a contactless card is very likely cheaper, and there is no 8-day pass in any case.

### Cited Findings
- Standard Singapore Tourist Pass prices: 1-Day S$17, 2-Day S$24, 3-Day S$29, 4-Day S$37, 5-Day S$45. Confirmed on both the official pass site and SimplyGo — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: types of passes](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/type-of-passes/); [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- The pass gives unlimited rides on public bus, MRT and LRT. It excludes premium and private services such as the Sentosa Express, RWS8, M2 Khatib Bus, express buses and other premium or niche bus services — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: types of passes](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/type-of-passes/)
- Multi-day passes must be used on consecutive days. Day 1 starts at first use and runs to the end of that day's public transport service — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: types of passes](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/type-of-passes/)
- Prices apply to adults and to children over 0.9 m in height — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: types of passes](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/type-of-passes/)
- Variant products: Singapore Tourist Pass Charm (Mr. Merlion, round) at S$22 for 1 day and S$34 for 3 days, sold from a SimplyGo vending machine at the Terminal 2 and 3 exit, open 24 hours — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: types of passes](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/type-of-passes/); [The Singapore Tourist Pass: where to buy](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/where-to-buy/)
- Variant products: Singapore Tourist Pass plus Changi Recommends Bundle at 1-Day S$19, 2-Day S$28, 3-Day S$48, 4-Day S$52, 5-Day S$60, adding Changi Travelite Insurance (3-day and above only), a 1-hour Xplore tour and S$5 Changi Travel Concierge credit. Non-refundable, and bundle contents can change without notice — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: types of passes](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/type-of-passes/)
- Where to buy at Changi: Terminal 2 Ticketing Service Centre daily 8am to 9pm plus automated kiosks beside it 5am to midnight; Terminal 3 automated kiosk beside the ticket office 5am to midnight. All 1- to 5-day passes are available at these points. No MRT ticketing location is listed for Terminal 1 — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: where to buy](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/where-to-buy/)
- 3-day pass only, 24 hours, at Changi: Cheers outlets (T1 Departure #02-05A, T2 Departure #02-24, T3 Arrival #01-24, T3 Departure #02-98, T4 Arrival #01-09), Prosegur Change Money counters in T1, T2 and T4, and Travelex counters in T1, T2 and T3 — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: where to buy](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/where-to-buy/)
- In the city, pass sales points with long hours include Bugis MRT ticket office (daily 10am to 9pm) with an automated kiosk beside it (5am to midnight), Orchard MRT ticket office (daily 8am to 9pm) with kiosk, Chinatown and Harbourfront ticket offices (daily 8am to 9pm). Jurong East MRT ticket office is daily 12pm to 8pm — [The Singapore Tourist Pass: where to buy](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/where-to-buy/)
- **Deposit position is contradictory and unresolved.** An older official brochure says each pass carries "a rental deposit of $10 which is refundable, so long as the card is returned within 5 days after the day of purchase" — [Singapore Tourist Pass brochure PDF](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/STP-A6-Brochure_V04.20_FA-editable.pdf) (dated around 2020). A more recent FAQ on the same site states the Singapore Tourist Pass is non-returnable and non-refundable — [The Singapore Tourist Pass FAQ](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/faq/singapore-tourist-pass/). SimplyGo describes the pass as having "a fixed cost per day" with the same price list and no mention of a deposit — [SimplyGo travel cards](https://simplygo.com.sg/travel-cards/)
- Current adult card fare range for basic bus and MRT/LRT is S$1.28 minimum (up to 3.2 km) to S$2.57 maximum (over 40.2 km) — [PTC fare structure](https://www.ptc.gov.sg/fare-regulation/bus-rail/fare-structure)

### Inferences
Break-even arithmetic, using the confirmed S$2.57 fare ceiling and a mid-range S$2.00 fare for comparison. Daily cost of each pass is the pass price divided by its days.

| Pass | Price | Cost per day | Journeys per day to break even at S$2.57 | At S$2.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-Day | S$17.00 | S$17.00 | 6.6, so 7 journeys | 8.5, so 9 journeys |
| 2-Day | S$24.00 | S$12.00 | 4.7, so 5 journeys | 6 journeys |
| 3-Day | S$29.00 | S$9.67 | 3.8, so 4 journeys | 4.8, so 5 journeys |
| 4-Day | S$37.00 | S$9.25 | 3.6, so 4 journeys | 4.6, so 5 journeys |
| 5-Day | S$45.00 | S$9.00 | 3.5, so 4 journeys | 4.5, so 5 journeys |

- Adjusting for the S$0.60 daily admin fee that a pass avoids, the thresholds drop very slightly. For the 3-day pass the break-even becomes (S$9.67 minus S$0.60) divided by S$2.57, which is 3.5 journeys a day.
- There is no 8-day pass. Covering 2 to 9 November would mean a 5-day pass plus a 3-day pass, S$74 per person, or S$148 for the couple. At 4 long journeys a day for 8 days at an average of S$2.40, pay-as-you-go costs about S$76.80 plus S$4.80 in admin fees, so about S$81.60 per person. At 3 journeys a day it falls to roughly S$62 per person. Pay-as-you-go wins at realistic levels of travel and only approaches parity at very heavy use.
- The pass is worth considering only for a single unusually heavy day, for example a day of hopping between five or six separate destinations. Buying a 1-day pass on such a day while otherwise tapping a bank card is possible but adds a queue at a ticket office.
- Given the deposit contradiction, the safest assumption is that the advertised price is the total payable and that nothing is refundable. Anyone who wants to rely on a refund should ask at the point of sale.

### Gaps
- The deposit question cannot be resolved from the sources found. The official site contradicts itself between an older brochure and a current FAQ, and SimplyGo does not mention a deposit at all. This should be verified at the counter.
- No confirmation found that 2026 prices differ from those listed, and no dated price-change notice was found. The prices above were live on the official sites when checked on 24 to 25 October 2026.

### Inferences
- Concrete worked examples from the table: a short feeder-bus hop or a two-station MRT trip costs S$1.28; a 10 km trip costs S$1.86; a 15 km trip costs S$2.07; a 20 km trip costs S$2.27; and any trip over 40.2 km costs the S$2.57 ceiling.
- The fare structure is strongly compressed at the top. The difference between a 20 km trip and a 45 km cross-island trip is only 30 cents. Long journeys are cheap in relative terms, which matters for a couple based at Hillview on the far west of the island.
- Hillview to the city centre is in the upper distance bands. Hillview (DT3) to Chinatown (DT19) is 16 Downtown Line stations, which at the line's average station spacing is roughly 20 km, putting it around the S$2.27 to S$2.40 bands. This is an estimate derived from station counts, not a published fare.
- A cross-island trip such as Changi Airport to Hillview or Pasir Ris to Tuas Link sits at or very near the S$2.57 ceiling.
- A small saving is available on weekday mornings. Tapping in at Hillview before 07:45 takes 50 cents off the fare, so a S$2.40 trip becomes S$1.90.
- If the trip dates were two months later the fares would be 13 cents higher per journey. Any fare figures the travellers read in a guide published after late December 2026 will be the higher set and will not apply to their November trip.

### Gaps
- The PTC fare structure page shows "last updated 28 January 2026" alongside a footer date of 23 October 2026, so the page's own date metadata is inconsistent. The figures match the 2025 review's stated 9 to 10 cent increase, which supports them being the current set, but a direct official confirmation that these exact amounts apply in November 2026 was not located.
- No published fare was found for the specific Hillview to Changi Airport or Hillview to city-centre pairs. The official fare calculator in the MyTransport.SG app should be used to confirm.
- Transfer rules were not verified in this research. The single distance-based table covering both bus and rail implies combined journeys are charged as one through-fare, but the specific transfer time limit and maximum number of transfers were not sourced.

## Operating hours, night buses and late-night services

### Takeaway
MRT services run from roughly 5:30am to just after midnight, with slightly later first trains on Sundays and public holidays. Singapore has had no night bus network since mid-2022, so between roughly midnight and 5:30am the only options are taxis, ride-hailing or a pre-booked airport transfer.

### Cited Findings

**Hillview station (DT3), the travellers' home station** — [LandTransportGuru: Hillview station](https://landtransportguru.net/hillview-station/)
- Towards DT1 Bukit Panjang (platform A): first train 6:13am weekdays and Saturdays, 6:33am Sundays and public holidays; last train 12:45am daily.
- Towards DT35 Expo (platform B): first train 5:34am weekdays and Saturdays, 5:54am Sundays and public holidays; last train 11:38pm daily.
- The source notes its own table layout is not fully explicit and advises confirming with SBS Transit or LTA.

**Changi Airport station (CG2)** — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html); [LandTransportGuru: Changi Airport station](https://landtransportguru.net/changi-airport-station/)
- First train to Tanah Merah: 5:31am Monday to Saturday, 5:59am Sundays and public holidays.
- Last train: 12:06am daily; passengers heading towards the city on the East West Line should board by 11:18pm to make the Tuas Link connection, and 12:06am for Pasir Ris connections.

**Night buses**
- NightRider and Nite Owl night bus services were suspended from 8 April 2020 and formally withdrawn on 30 June 2022, along with Chinatown Direct and some Resorts World Sentosa routes — [LandTransportGuru: withdrawal of night and leisure bus services](https://landtransportguru.net/withdrawal-of-night-and-leisure-bus-services/)
- LTA cited low ridership, alternative options, financial considerations and resource allocation. In March 2025 the Ministry of Transport disclosed that NightRider and Nite Owl buses had run with an average of about 10 passengers per trip — [LandTransportGuru: withdrawal of night and leisure bus services](https://landtransportguru.net/withdrawal-of-night-and-leisure-bus-services/)
- A plan to renumber NR1 as N1 never happened because night services stayed suspended. Private operator A&S Transit launched two night services in 2023 — [LandTransportGuru: Defunct NightRider services](https://landtransportguru.net/nightrider-services/)
- Changi Airport's own guidance confirms the position: "Most bus and MRT services stop overnight," and advises checking first and last bus times on airport bus boards or at mytransport.sg — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)

### Inferences
- The practical last-train window for getting back to Hillview is the key constraint on evening plans. The last Hillview-bound Downtown Line train passes Hillview at 12:45am, so boarding in the city centre by around midnight should be safe, but this should be checked on the day in the app.
- Hillview's Expo-bound last train at 11:38pm matters less for a visitor but caps late evening travel eastwards.
- For a couple out in the city in the evening, the realistic plan is MRT out and MRT back until about midnight, then a taxi or Grab after that. There is no cheap late-night public option.
- The two A&S Transit night services run by a private operator are unlikely to be useful for a Hillview base and were not researched further. They are not part of the public bus network and a Tourist Pass would not cover them.

### Gaps
- The exact last train times for the Hillview-bound Downtown Line at intermediate city stations were not collected. Only the Hillview station times are sourced here.
- Route and coverage of the two A&S Transit night services were not established.

## How public buses work for a visitor

### Takeaway
Board at the front door, tap the card on the reader as you board, and tap again on the reader by the exit door when you get off. Forgetting to tap out charges the maximum fare. The LTA's own MyTransport.SG app is the authoritative source for live bus arrival times.

### Cited Findings
- Payment on buses accepts the same options as the MRT: contactless Mastercard, Visa or NETS bank cards, mobile wallets, stored-value EZ-Link cards, and the Singapore Tourist Pass. Cash is accepted but exact fare only, and no change is given — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)
- Fares are distance-based on a single table shared with rail, so the fare depends on how far you travel, not on a flat per-ride charge — [PTC fare structure](https://www.ptc.gov.sg/fare-regulation/bus-rail/fare-structure)
- Cash bus fares are substantially higher than card fares: S$2.10 against S$1.28 for a short trip, and S$3.20 against S$2.57 at the top of the range — [PTC fare structure](https://www.ptc.gov.sg/fare-regulation/bus-rail/fare-structure)
- Express bus services carry a premium over basic fares and are excluded from the Singapore Tourist Pass — [PTC: Fare Review Exercise 2025](https://www.ptc.gov.sg/media-centre/newsroom/fare-review-exercise-2025/); [The Singapore Tourist Pass: types of passes](https://thesingaporetouristpass.com.sg/type-of-passes/)

### Inferences

## Changi Airport to Hillview

### Takeaway
The MRT route is Changi Airport to Expo, then the Downtown Line all the way west towards Bukit Panjang, alighting at Hillview. Allow about 1 hour 15 minutes and expect a fare at or near the S$2.57 ceiling. A taxi or Grab is roughly 40 minutes. For a late arrival after the last train, the Changi Ground Transport Concierge offers a fixed S$70 transfer for a 4-seater, available 24 hours, which is a known price rather than a surge-exposed one.

### Cited Findings

**MRT route**
- Changi Airport's own guidance for Downtown Line destinations: "take the train to Expo (CG1/DT35), then the Downtown Line toward Bukit Panjang (BP6/DT1)" — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)
- Hillview is DT3 on the Downtown Line, between Hume (DT4) and Cashew (DT2), three stations from the Bukit Panjang terminus — [LandTransportGuru: Hillview station](https://landtransportguru.net/hillview-station/)
- Expo is the interchange between the Downtown Line and the East West Line, with a B1 transfer linkway between the two concourses — [Expo MRT station, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_MRT_station)
- Journey time estimates conflict and no authoritative figure was found. Rome2Rio gives about 1 hour 12 minutes by public transport via Expo, though its pages are 1,300 to 2,400 days old. One route planner gives 31 stations on the Downtown Line plus a change at Expo at an estimated 1 hour 37 minutes, and a second route on the same planner gives about 1 hour 13 minutes with one transfer — [Rome2Rio: Changi Airport MRT to Hillview](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Changi-Airport-MRT-Station/Hillview-Singapore); [mynetbizz route planner](https://www.mynetbizz.com/MRT/To-Changi-Airport-MRT-Station/Hillview-To-Changi-Airport-MRT-Station.cfm)
- The Expo to Changi Airport leg is short, variously given as about 3 minutes and about 5 minutes — [Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Changi-Airport-MRT-Station/Hillview-Singapore)
- No published fare was found for this specific pair. Third-party guides give a range of roughly S$2 to S$4, which is too loose to use — [Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Changi-Airport-MRT-Station/Hillview-Singapore)

**Which terminal, and getting to the station**
- Changi Airport MRT station serves Terminals 2 and 3 directly, with an exit at each end — [LandTransportGuru: Changi Airport station](https://landtransportguru.net/changi-airport-station/)

Passengers heading to the city on the East West Line should board by 11:18pm to make the Tuas Link connection — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html); [LandTransportGuru: Changi Airport station](https://landtransportguru.net/changi-airport-station/)

**Ride-hailing**
- Licensed providers are Grab, Gojek, Zig, Tada Mobility, Ryde and Geolah, with pick-up at the Arrival and Ride-Hailing pick-up points at Terminals 1 to 4 — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)

- Changi's current "leaving the airport" page does not mention Terminal 5 at all — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)

### Inferences
- **Terminal 5 is irrelevant to a November 2026 trip.** It will not be open. Arrivals will be at Terminals 1 to 4 and all planning should assume four terminals.
- **MRT fare estimate.** Fares are set by the distance table and a Changi to Hillview trip crosses the island. Via Expo and the full Downtown Line the rail distance is roughly 44 km, which is past the 40.2 km band and therefore the S$2.57 ceiling. Even the shortest plausible routing is above 30 km, which puts the fare at S$2.47 or higher. Plan on S$2.47 to S$2.57 per person, about S$5.14 for the couple. This is derived from the distance table and station counts, not a published fare, so confirm in the MyTransport.SG fare calculator.
- **MRT time estimate.** Taking the sources together, about 1 hour 15 minutes is the reasonable planning figure for train time, plus walking from the terminal to the station, the transfer at Expo, and waiting. Door to door from the aircraft, allow 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours. The 1 hour 37 minute figure from one planner probably reflects the all-Downtown-Line routing with 31 stations, which is slower than changing at Tanah Merah onto the East West Line and then onto the Downtown Line in the city.
- **The MRT route is awkward with luggage.** It involves at least one and possibly two transfers, and the full Downtown Line routing stops 32 times. For a couple arriving with suitcases, the S$5.14 saving over a taxi is real but the convenience cost is high.
- **Taxi and Grab cost estimate.** Changi to Hillview is further west than the city centre, so the metered fare should sit above Changi's quoted S$25 to S$45 city range. Allowing for roughly 10 to 12 km of extra distance at the published S$0.27 per 350 m beyond 10 km, plus the S$6 or S$8 airport surcharge, expect roughly S$45 to S$65 by metered taxi outside peak and midnight hours. A midnight arrival between 12:00am and 5:59am adds 50 percent of the metered fare, which could push the total towards S$70 to S$90. Grab and similar apps quote a fixed fare up front that tends to track the taxi range but rises under surge. These are calculated estimates, not quoted prices.
- **Late-night arrivals.** Any arrival where the travellers clear immigration and baggage after about 11:00pm should be treated as a no-MRT arrival. The last Changi train is 12:06am but the Downtown Line connection timing at Expo is not published, and cutting it fine with luggage at an unfamiliar interchange is a poor plan. The Ground Transport Concierge fixed fare of S$70 for a 4-seater, available 24 hours and bookable before departure through the Changi App, is the lowest-risk late-night option because the price is known in advance and is not exposed to the 50 percent midnight surcharge or to ride-hailing surge. At the top of the estimated taxi range it may also be no more expensive.
- **If arriving at Terminal 4**, add the shuttle bus to Terminal 2 before the MRT. For a late or luggage-heavy arrival this tips the balance further towards a car.

### Gaps
- **The last usable train from Changi Airport to Hillview is not established.** Neither the Changi page nor the station page publishes the last Downtown Line connection at Expo. Changi publishes 11:18pm only for the Tuas Link connection on the East West Line. This should be checked in the MyTransport.SG journey planner on the day.
- No published fare for the Changi Airport to Hillview pair was found. The figure above is derived from the distance table.
- No quoted taxi or Grab fare for Changi to Hillview was found. The range above is calculated from the published metered rates and surcharges.
- Terminal 4 shuttle bus frequency and operating hours were not found.
- ComfortDelGro's exact flag-down for the taxi types likely to be at the airport rank is published, but the distance breakdown for a specific 35 km journey was not computed from an official calculator.

## Taxi and Grab cost ranges and surcharges

### Takeaway
ComfortDelGro metered taxis start at S$4.60 to S$4.80 for the first kilometre and add S$0.27 per 400 m to 10 km, then S$0.27 per 350 m. Surcharges stack on the meter: 25 percent at peak, 50 percent between midnight and 5:59am, and S$6 or S$8 from the airport. A midnight airport run can therefore cost half again as much as the same trip at noon.

### Cited Findings
- Flag-down fares on the ComfortDelGro fare page: S$4.60 for the first kilometre on Toyota Prius and Hyundai Ioniq Hybrid taxis, S$4.80 on electric models, S$4.80 for Sienta and Noah 6-seaters, and S$5.20 or S$5.50 for limousine cabs depending on model — [ComfortDelGro Taxi fares](https://www.cdgtaxi.com.sg/fares/)
- Beyond the first kilometre: S$0.27 per 400 m up to 10 km, then S$0.27 per 350 m beyond 10 km. Waiting time is S$0.27 per 45 seconds — [ComfortDelGro Taxi fares](https://www.cdgtaxi.com.sg/fares/)
- A temporary S$0.01 increase to the Distance-Time Rate applies from 24 March 2026. **The end date conflicts across sources:** one copy of the operator's page gives 31 July 2026, a second copy gives 30 September 2026, and a March 2026 news report gave 31 May 2026 — [ComfortDelGro Taxi fares](https://www.cdgtaxi.com.sg/fares/); [Malay Mail: ComfortDelGro hikes metered fares](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2026/03/18/comfortdelgro-hikes-metered-fares-introduces-booking-fee-in-singapore-from-march-24/213028)
- Airport surcharge: S$8 Monday to Sunday 5:00pm to 11:59pm, S$6 at all other times — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)
- Midnight surcharge: 50 percent of the metered fare, Monday to Sunday 12:00am to 5:59am — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)
- Peak-hour surcharge: 25 percent of the metered fare, Monday to Friday excluding public holidays 6:00am to 9:29am; Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays 10:00am to 1:59pm; and Monday to Sunday 5:00pm to 11:59pm — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)
- Flag-down and distance charges vary by taxi company — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)
- Changi to the city: about 30 minutes, roughly S$25 to S$45 — [Changi Airport: leaving the airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/transport/leaving-the-airport.html)
- Surcharges stack on the meter and also include a city or airport location charge, a credit-card admin fee, and a booking charge for app or hotline bookings. One guide puts the location charge at a flat S$2 to S$8 for certain pick-up spots — [ComfortDelGro Taxi fares](https://www.cdgtaxi.com.sg/fares/); [DollarsAndSense](https://dollarsandsense.sg/how-much-have-different-taxi-companies-raised-fares-amid-higher-oil-prices/)
- Zig booking fee: 50 cents for trips below S$15 and 80 cents for fares of S$15 and above — [DollarsAndSense](https://dollarsandsense.sg/how-much-have-different-taxi-companies-raised-fares-amid-higher-oil-prices/)
- One travel blog cites a S$3.90 flag-down and S$0.33 increments, which does not match the operator's published figures and should be disregarded — flagged as a conflict in search results against [ComfortDelGro Taxi fares](https://www.cdgtaxi.com.sg/fares/)

### Inferences
- A short Hillview-area trip of 3 to 5 km by metered taxi comes to roughly S$6 to S$8 before surcharges, using the S$4.60 flag-down plus S$0.27 per 400 m.
- A Hillview to city centre taxi of roughly 20 km comes to roughly S$4.60 plus S$6.08 for the 1 to 10 km segment plus S$7.71 for the 10 to 20 km segment, so about S$18 before surcharges. In the 5:00pm to 11:59pm peak window the 25 percent surcharge takes it to about S$23. After midnight the 50 percent surcharge takes it to about S$27.
- Returning to Hillview after midnight is the worst-value taxi window. The 50 percent midnight surcharge applies exactly when the MRT has stopped. A journey home before 12:45am by Downtown Line costs about S$2.40 per person instead of about S$27 for the pair.
- Grab and other ride-hailing apps quote a fixed price before booking, which removes surcharge uncertainty but exposes the traveller to surge pricing in the rain and at closing time. Comparing a Grab quote against the metered estimates above is the practical way to decide.
- The ERP electronic road pricing charge is passed through to the passenger in a metered taxi and is normally built into a ride-hailing quote. It is relevant mainly for weekday trips into the central area and should be small for evening and weekend leisure travel.

### Gaps
- No official list of the city-area or location surcharge amounts and the specific pick-up points they apply to was found. The S$2 to S$8 range comes from a secondary guide.
- **ERP charges were not researched.** No source was found on current ERP gantry rates, operating hours, or how they are passed on to taxi and ride-hailing passengers. This is a genuine gap.
- No sourced Grab fare ranges for any specific Singapore trip were found. Grab does not publish a fare table.
- The end date of the S$0.01 temporary distance-time increase is contradictory across three sources, so whether it is still in force in November 2026 is unresolved.

## Accessibility, luggage, eating and drinking, and fines

### Takeaway
Eating or drinking carries a fine of up to S$500 on trains and, since 23 September 2026, on buses as well under new regulations. That bus rule is recent and many guidebooks will not mention it. Hillview station has lift access and barrier-free entry at both exits.

### Cited Findings

**New bus conduct rules, in force since 23 September 2026**
- The Bus Services Industry (Control of Conduct and Activities – Public Bus) Regulations 2026 and the equivalent Bus Interchange regulations took effect on 23 September 2026 — [TheSmartLocal: bus rules Singapore](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/bus-rules-singapore/); [Planetizen: eating and littering on Singapore buses now a fineable offense](https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/09/138512-eating-littering-singapore-buses-now-fineable-offense)
- Eating or drinking on board a public bus now carries a fine of up to S$500. Other S$500 offences include playing loud audio and putting feet on seats — [TheSmartLocal: bus rules Singapore](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/bus-rules-singapore/); [Planetizen](https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/09/138512-eating-littering-singapore-buses-now-fineable-offense)
- Causing danger or obstruction at bus interchanges and terminals, or soiling any part of a bus, interchange or terminal, can lead to a fine of up to S$5,000 — [TheSmartLocal: bus rules Singapore](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/bus-rules-singapore/)
- Enforcement is graduated. Bus drivers may first advise a passenger to stop, and may require someone who refuses to comply to leave the bus. LTA said it will review the rules' effectiveness after six months — [TheSmartLocal: bus rules Singapore](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/bus-rules-singapore/)

**MRT rules and fines**
- Smoking: the Rapid Transit Systems Regulations impose a duty not to smoke or carry lighted tobacco products where prohibited by notice. Conviction carries a fine not exceeding S$1,000. The offence may be compounded by the Chief Executive of LTA for the lower of half the maximum fine or S$5,000 — [Tobacco Control Laws: Rapid Transit Systems Act, Singapore](https://www.tobaccocontrollaws.org/laws/rapid-transit-systems-act-singapore)
- Eating and drinking on the MRT is banned with a fine of up to S$500 according to multiple travel guides, including a prohibition on drinking plain water in trains and stations. These are secondary sources, not the legal text — [Silverdoor: unique laws in Singapore](https://silverdoor.com/blog/12-unique-laws-in-singapore-you-should-know-before-you-visit-)
- Durian is banned from the MRT. One travel article says bringing a durian aboard could mean a S$500 fine. Another source says the fruit is also banned from most hotels and many indoor public spaces. These are travel blogs, not authoritative — [Business Insider India: ways to embarrass yourself in Singapore](https://www.businessinsider.in/9-ways-to-embarrass-yourself-in-Singapore/articleshow/49149133.cms)
- One guide gives a smoking figure of S$200 for prohibited areas, which contradicts the S$1,000 statutory maximum above — noted as a conflict against [Tobacco Control Laws](https://www.tobaccocontrollaws.org/laws/rapid-transit-systems-act-singapore)

**Accessibility at Hillview**
- Escalators and lifts link both exits to the concourse. Both ground-level exits provide barrier-free access — [LandTransportGuru: Hillview station](https://landtransportguru.net/hillview-station/)
- The concourse at B2 has faregates, ticketing service kiosks, a passenger service centre, public toilets and an ATM. A taxi stand (H21) and a pick-up and drop-off point are at Exit B, with a further pick-up and drop-off point at Exit A — [LandTransportGuru: Hillview station](https://landtransportguru.net/hillview-station/)
- The station is a designated Civil Defence shelter, which is why it is deep and the lift ride is long — [LandTransportGuru: Hillview station](https://landtransportguru.net/hillview-station/)

### Inferences
