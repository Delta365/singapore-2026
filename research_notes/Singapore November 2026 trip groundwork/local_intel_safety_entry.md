## 1. Entry requirements: visa, timing risk, SG Arrival Card, automated clearance

### Takeaway
Indian ordinary passport holders need a Singapore visa, applied for through an authorised visa agent or a Singapore mission (not at the border), with a usual processing time of 3 to 5 working days. Applying on Monday 26 October for a Monday 2 November arrival is very tight and could fail; the SG Arrival Card can be submitted from Saturday 31 October.

### Cited Findings
- ICA lists India among countries whose travel documents require a visa to enter Singapore; a visa does not guarantee entry, and the final pass is granted by ICA officers at the checkpoint ([ICA visa requirements](https://www.ica.gov.sg/enter-transit-depart/entering-singapore/visa_requirements)).
- Visa applications cannot be made at immigration counters. Applicants without a local contact holding Singpass or Corppass apply through an authorised visa agent appointed by a Singapore Overseas Mission, or at the nearest Singapore mission ([ICA](https://www.ica.gov.sg/enter-transit-depart/entering-singapore/visa_requirements)).
- Travellers need a valid, individually issued travel document; collective travel documents are not accepted ([ICA](https://www.ica.gov.sg/enter-transit-depart/entering-singapore/visa_requirements)).
- India is named among nationalities that "may be eligible" for the Visa-Free Transit Facility (VFTF) ([ICA](https://www.ica.gov.sg/enter-transit-depart/entering-singapore/visa_requirements)). The VFTF detail page returned 404 this session. VFTF is a transit concession and does not apply to a round trip India to Singapore to India.
- Processing time: usually 3 to 5 working days after submission to an authorised agent; the submission day, weekends and public holidays are excluded; peak periods take longer ([Visa2Fly](https://visa2fly.com/blog/Singapore-Tourist-Visa-Processing-Time-for-Indians); [visaguideindia](https://visaguideindia.com/singapore-tourist-visa-processing-time-indians/)). No visa on arrival and no visa-free entry for Indians ([BankBazaar](https://www.bankbazaar.com/visa/singapore-visa-fees-for-indians.html)).
- Fee: sources conflict. One says the Consulate-General in Mumbai fixed the government fee at Rs 2,100 per application from 1 January 2026 ([visaguideindia](https://visaguideindia.com/singapore-tourist-visa-for-indians/)); others cite Rs 1,900 or S$30 plus service charges ([BankBazaar](https://www.bankbazaar.com/visa/singapore-visa-fees-for-indians.html); [Dream Travel Yaari](https://www.dreamtravelyaari.com/blog-details/singapore-visa-for-indians)). Agent fees add roughly Rs 650 to Rs 1,300 (secondary sources, as summarised in search results).
- SG Arrival Card (SGAC): every traveller must submit it within three days before arrival, free of charge, only via ICA's SGAC e-Service or the MyICA app ([Changi Airport, immigration and customs](https://changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/arriving/immigration-and-customs.html)). Example: for a 30 June arrival, submission opens 28 June ([IEEE APPEEC 2026 visa page](https://attend.ieee.org/appeec-2026/visa)).
- Automated clearance: all travellers, including first-time visitors, can use automated clearance on arrival at Changi with no prior enrolment, as long as they hold an ICAO-compliant passport ([Changi Airport](https://changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/arriving/immigration-and-customs.html)). The e-Pass (electronic visit pass) is sent to the email address given on the SGAC ([MustShareNews](https://mustsharenews.com/ica-automated-lanes/)). ICA also advises submitting the SGAC early and not covering the face at the lanes ([MustShareNews](https://mustsharenews.com/ica-automated-lanes/)).

### Inferences
- Visa timing arithmetic (calendar check: 25 October 2026 is a Sunday). If the application reaches an agent on Monday 26 October, working days counted are Tuesday 27 to Friday 30 October (4 days) and Monday 2 November (5th day). A 3-day result arrives Thursday 29 October; a 5-day result arrives on the travel day itself, which is too late for an evening arrival only if the agent has not yet released it by morning. Any delay, document query or mission backlog would mean missing the flight. Conclusion: it is possible but not safe. If the visa is not already issued, the couple should apply at the very first opportunity on 26 October, use an agent that offers priority handling if available, and hold refundable or changeable bookings. Applications made less than ten days before travel carry a real risk of failure.
- Fee per person likely totals about Rs 2,500 to Rs 3,500 through a mainstream agent (about S$33 to S$47), but confirm with the chosen agent.
- For a 2 November arrival, the SGAC window opens on Saturday 31 October (Singapore time). Each traveller files separately. Use the same email you check on your phone, because the e-Pass arrives there.
- Do not use third-party SGAC "service" sites that charge a fee: the official channels are free.

### Gaps
- Official passport-validity rule: ICA's page did not state it this session. Widely reported guidance is at least six months' validity from the date of arrival; confirm on ICA's entry-requirements page.
- Official e-visa validity and length of stay (commonly reported as single or multiple entry, stays of up to 30 days) were not confirmed from an official source.
- Exact SGAC fields (passport details, flight, Singapore address, contact details, health declaration) not verified from the ICA page this session.
- Document list for the e-visa (Form 14A, photo, passport copy, return ticket, hotel booking, bank statements) is standard agent practice but was not confirmed from an official source.
- No 2025 or 2026 rule change for Indian visitors (such as visa-free entry) was found. A "visa-free" headline exists on a 2026 blog ([JNP Travel](https://jnptravel.com/blog/india-singapore-travel-guide-2026-visa-free)), but other sources say there is no visa-free entry; treat the blog headline as unreliable.

## 2. Customs: alcohol, tobacco, gum, medicines, vapes, cash

### Takeaway
There is no duty-free allowance for any tobacco, alcohol concessions apply only after 48 hours away and are capped (sources conflict on the exact mix), cash over S$20,000 must be declared, and vapes are now a serious offence with fines up to S$10,000 under a law in force since 1 May 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Tobacco: no duty-free concession since January 2023; all tobacco is dutiable ([EntryBrief](https://entrybrief.com/customs/singapore/); [Zurich Kotak](https://www.zurichkotak.com/knowledge-center/travel-insurance/singapore-customs-and-duty-free)).
- Alcohol: the concession requires 48 hours or more away from Singapore and does not apply on arrival from Malaysia. Sources conflict on quantity: options such as 1L spirits plus 1L wine, or 2L wine, or 2L beer (up to 2L total) per [World Travel Guide](https://www.worldtravelguide.net/guides/asia/singapore/money-duty-free) and [Zurich Kotak](https://www.zurichkotak.com/knowledge-center/travel-insurance/singapore-customs-and-duty-free); another source states 1L spirits, 1L wine and 1L beer (3L) and a third states 1L total ([Goods Across Borders](https://www.goodsacrossborders.com/traveling/allowances/singapore)). The official Customs page returned 404 this session.
- Cash: physical currency and bearer negotiable instruments over S$20,000 in total must be declared (Form NP 727, for trips from 13 May 2024), submitted online within 72 hours before arrival or departure; failure can bring up to three years' jail, fines up to S$50,000, or both ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/07/03/singapores-new-s20000-cash-declaration-rules-what-malaysians-need-to-know/142583); [Yahoo/Malay Mail](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/singapore-20-000-cash-declaration-035601626.html)).
- Vapes: under the Tobacco and Vaporisers Control Act, in force from 1 May 2026, people found vaping can be fined up to S$10,000; etomidate (Kpod) vapes carry up to S$20,000, up to 10 years' jail, or both ([Tobacco Asia](https://www.tobaccoasia.com/news/singapore-escalates-vaping-crackdown/)). Short-term visitors who re-offend are banned from re-entering Singapore ([Tobacco Asia](https://www.tobaccoasia.com/news/singapore-escalates-vaping-crackdown/); [Loyalty Lobby](https://loyaltylobby.com/2025/08/29/singapore-bans-vapes-visitors-bringing-them-in-face-entry-ban-possible-caning-and-up-to-20-years-in-jail/)).
- Enforcement: ICA recorded 56 travellers with vaporisers in four days in late July 2026, about 77% of them short-term visitors, and seized more than 444 items ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/25/56-travellers-caught-entering-singapore-with-vapes-over-four-days-more-than-444-items-seized)). Transit passengers with vapes may be charged even without clearing immigration ([Goods Across Borders](https://goodsacrossborders.com/traveling/can-i-bring/vape-e-cigarette/to/singapore)).
- Source conflict: an April 2026 customs guide still quotes up to S$2,000 for a first offence ([Goods Across Borders](https://goodsacrossborders.com/traveling/can-i-bring/vape-e-cigarette/to/singapore)); this appears to predate the 1 May 2026 Act.

### Inferences
- Simplest rule for the couple: bring no cigarettes, no vapes or e-cigarettes of any kind, and at most one or two bottles of duty-free alcohol bought after leaving India; check the official Customs page for the exact combination.
- Carry prescription medicines in original packaging with a doctor's letter.

### Gaps
- Chewing gum rule (import for sale is banned; small personal quantities are generally tolerated, and therapeutic gum is allowed) was not confirmed from an official source this session.
- Controlled medicines: which common Indian prescriptions (for example codeine-containing cough syrups, some sleeping pills, strong painkillers, ADHD medication) need HSA approval was not researched; the HSA "bringing personal medication" page should be checked. Codeine syrups commonly sold in India are a known risk item.
- Indian customs limits on return (duty-free allowance into India) are outside scope and not researched.

## 3. Safety and scams

### Takeaway
Singapore is very safe for visitors, and the main 2026 risks are digital: phishing SMS and pop-ups impersonating government agencies. Physical tourist-trap scams exist but are not documented in official 2026 alerts.

### Cited Findings
- Police advisory (10 February 2026): fake LTA SMS demanding unpaid tolls, at least 10 cases since 27 January 2026 with losses of at least S$24,000; police state LTA messages do not contain payment links and official government SMS come from the single "gov.sg" sender ID ([Singapore Police Force](https://www.police.gov.sg/Media-Hub/News/2026/02/20260210_police_advisory_on_phishing_scams_targeting_singaporean_travellers_to_malaysia)).
- Police advisory (24 February 2026): fake pop-ups impersonating the SPF claiming a computer is locked; the SPF cannot lock or freeze personal devices ([SPF](https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/news/2026/02/20260224_police_advisory_on_phishing_scams_pop_up_alerts_impersonating_the_singapore_police_force)).
- ScamShield app (iOS and Android) blocks scam calls, detects scam messages and allows reporting; the 24/7 ScamShield Helpline is 1799 ([Open Government Products](https://www.open.gov.sg/products/scamshield); [Smart Nation](https://smartnation.gov.sg/initiatives/scamshield)).
- An Anti-Scam Centre operation with five banks (1 March to 30 April 2026) disrupted over 500 scams and averted more than S$37 million in losses ([SPF](https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/news/2026/05/20260512_joint_operation_between_the_anti-scam_centre_and_five_partnering_banks)).
- Fake Singtel SMS phishing has been a recurring pattern ([SPF 2023 advisory](https://www.police.gov.sg/media-hub/news/2023/20230628_police_advisory_on_phishing_scams_involving_fake_singtel_sms)).
- An Indian insurer's blog lists common tourist scams in Singapore and the 1799 hotline, but notes it may contain outdated data ([ICICI Lombard](https://www.icicilombard.com/blogs/travel-insurance/ti/common-tourist-scams-in-singapore)).

### Inferences
- With a local or tourist SIM, expect scam SMS. Any SMS about parcels, tolls, fines, SGAC "fees" or visa problems with a link is almost certainly fake. Only "gov.sg" is a genuine government sender.
- Buy attraction tickets only from official sites, the attraction counter, or established platforms (Klook, Pelago); avoid Facebook or Telegram resellers.
- At seafood restaurants, crab and lobster are priced per 100g or per kg ("seasonal price"); ask for the weight and total price before the crab is cooked.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 official alert specifically about tourist-trap restaurant pricing, ticket resellers, or shopping-area touts (for example at Sim Lim Square electronics shops, historically the subject of CASE complaints) was found this session.
- No official pickpocketing data found. General traveller reporting suggests low risk, with care at crowded spots (Deepavali in Little India, Chinatown, MRT at peak hours).

## 4. Rules and fines visitors break by accident

### Takeaway
Eating and drinking (even plain water) are now fineable on public buses and at interchanges from 23 September 2026, matching long-standing MRT rules; littering, including leaving trays and tissues at hawker tables, is enforced.

### Cited Findings
- From 23 September 2026, the Bus Services Industry (Control of Conduct and Activities, Public Bus) Regulations 2026 and the Bus Interchange regulations make eating or drinking on a bus, feet on seats and loud audio offences with a maximum fine of S$500; littering or soiling a bus carries up to S$5,000; rules also cover bus interchanges and terminals and broadly align with rail rules ([Planetizen](https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/09/138512-eating-littering-singapore-buses-now-fineable-offense); [TheSmartLocal](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/bus-rules-singapore/); [Bernama](https://bernama.com/en/world/news.php?id=2609995)).
- The fine applies even to drinking plain water ([YourLifeChoices](https://www.yourlifechoices.com.au/travel/the-commuter-habit-that-could-now-empty-your-wallet/)).
- Date conflict: one outlet gives 24 September 2026 ([Travel Tomorrow](https://traveltomorrow.com/?p=186751)); most, including LTA-based reports, give 23 September. Either way the rule is in force for November.
- Hawker tray return: leaving litter on tables is enforceable as littering under Section 17(1) of the Environmental Public Health Act; enforcement began 1 September 2021 at hawker centres and from 1 January 2022 at coffee shops and food courts; first offenders who refuse officers' advice get a written warning, and repeat offenders face fines (S$300 reported) or court; covered items include tissues, wet wipes, straws, wrappers and cans ([Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2021/08/fines-for-not-clearing-trays-at-hawker-centres//amp); [Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.com/diners-fail-clear-table-hawker-centre-face-fine-1-september-nea-120626355.html); [MSE](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/written-reply-to-pq-on-fines-for-not-returning-trays/)).
- Enforcement still happens: a report described an elderly man receiving a warning at Chinatown Complex ([MustShareNews](https://mustsharenews.com/warning-not-returning-tray/)).

### Inferences
- Do not sip water on the MRT or bus, including the Downtown Line from Hillview. Drink before boarding.
- Return trays to the marked racks (separate racks for halal and non-halal crockery at many centres) and take tissue packets with you; a tissue packet left on a seat signals the seat is "choped" (reserved).

### Gaps
- MRT fines (eating/drinking up to S$500; durians prohibited on trains and buses), littering fines for first offenders (commonly S$300), jaywalking penalties, designated smoking areas (smoking is banned in most public places and in Orchard Road outside yellow-box zones), drone rules (CAAS permits; no-fly zones near airports and many areas) and photography restrictions (no photos at protected places, military areas, some immigration checkpoints) were not verified from official sources this session. These are widely known rules; the report writer should present them as standard and advise checking LTA, NEA and CAAS.

## 5. Cultural and religious etiquette

### Takeaway
Cover shoulders and knees at all places of worship, remove shoes at Hindu temples and mosques, and plan the Sultan Mosque visit outside Friday midday prayers. Treat Kampong Lorong Buangkok as a private village: stay on lanes and ask before photographing homes.

### Cited Findings
- Sultan Mosque (Masjid Sultan) visiting hours: Saturday to Thursday 10:00 to 12:00 and 14:00 to 16:00; Friday 14:30 to 16:00; closed for prayers 12:30 to 14:00 and 16:00 to 17:00 ([Holidify](https://www.holidify.com/places/singapore/sultan-mosque-singapore-sightseeing-121125.html); [Forevervacation](https://forevervacation.com/singapore/masjid-sultan)). These are secondary sources and not dated for 2026.
- Complimentary robes are available at the counter, first come first served ([mosque text quoted on Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/--d317468.html)). Dress: sleeved tops and long trousers for men; long sleeves and ankle-length bottoms for women; visitors may not enter the prayer hall unless accompanied by a mosque official ([Holidify](https://www.holidify.com/places/singapore/sultan-mosque-singapore-sightseeing-121125.html)).
- Kampong Lorong Buangkok: visitors should respect villagers' private space ([Remember Singapore](https://remembersingapore.org/kampong-lorong-buangkok/)); avoid photos inside houses unless invited, and photograph exteriors only ([Seek Sophie](https://www.seeksophie.com/en/magazine/a/a-trip-to-singapores-last-kampong--lorong-buangkok)); the land is still privately owned ([Seek Sophie](https://www.seeksophie.com/en/magazine/a/a-trip-to-singapores-last-kampong--lorong-buangkok)); villagers were reported in 2012 to charge entrance fees to sightseers ([SMU News](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2012/09/02/buangkok-villagers-charge-entrance-fees)), and a 2017 report described residents as wary of visitors ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/life/2017/10/07/at-singapores-last-kampung-villagers-cast-a-wary-eye-on-the-future/1481727)).

### Inferences
- Kampong visit: go in daylight, keep voices low, keep to the paths, do not enter compounds, do not fly drones, greet residents if they make eye contact, and ask before photographing people.
- Hindu temples (Sri Mariamman in Chinatown, Sri Veeramakaliamman and Sri Srinivasa Perumal in Little India): shoes off at the entrance; on and around Deepavali (Sunday 8 November) expect very large crowds in Little India, so visit temples on an earlier morning.
- Avoid the Sultan Mosque on Friday 6 November before 14:30.

### Gaps
- Official Masjid Sultan site was not fetched; confirm hours there (and whether hours change on Deepavali week; Deepavali is a Hindu festival and should not affect the mosque).
- Buddhist and Chinese temple rules (Buddha Tooth Relic Temple dress code with shawls provided, no photography in some halls) not verified this session.
- Tipping and service charges: not researched with a source. Widely known: tipping is not expected; restaurants add 10% service charge plus 9% GST (GST rose to 9% in 2024); hawkers do not take tips.
- Queueing norms: not sourced (queues are orderly; stand left on escalators with walking on the right is the common practice; let passengers alight first on the MRT).

## 6. Connectivity

### Takeaway
Tourist SIMs and eSIMs from Singtel, StarHub and M1 start at about S$12 (about Rs 900) at Changi for 100GB over 14 to 15 days, which beats Indian roaming packs for an eight-day trip.

## 9. Health: haze, dengue, heat, water

### Takeaway
2026 is a haze year. PSI was in the unhealthy range (above 100) repeatedly from September to at least 8 October, with a strong El Nino delaying the end of the dry season to late October or early November, so pack N95 masks and check NEA readings daily.

### Cited Findings
- PSI entered the unhealthy range in September 2026 for the first time since October 2023; the year's highest reading was 167 in central Singapore on 29 September (another outlet gives 164) ([The Star, 7 October 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/10/07/haze-in-singapore-psi-in-unhealthy-range-across-the-island-pm25-levels-elevated-in-all-regions)).
- In early October 2026, 24-hour PSI stood at 129 in the centre, 118 in the east and 115 in the west ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/10/07/haze-in-singapore-psi-in-unhealthy-range-across-the-island-pm25-levels-elevated-in-all-regions)); three regions recorded unhealthy air for over 24 hours ([Malay Mail, 7 October 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2026/10/07/haze-blankets-singapore-with-three-regions-recording-unhealthy-air-for-over-24-hours/237997)); haze continued on 8 October ([The Star, 8 October](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/10/08/singapore-shrouded-in-haze-psi-readings-in-the-unhealthy-range-across-the-island)).
- MSS: strong El Nino events delay the retreat of the dry season; the transition is expected only in late October or early November 2026; El Nino could reduce rainfall and raise temperatures over October and November ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/10/07/haze-in-singapore-psi-in-unhealthy-range-across-the-island-pm25-levels-elevated-in-all-regions); [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=293500)).

### Inferences

- Dengue: use repellent (DEET or picaridin) for the Zoo, wetlands (Sungei Buloh), MacRitchie and Bukit Timah trails near Hillview, and Kampong Buangkok.

## 10. Practical details: power, payments, ATMs, UPI

### Takeaway
Singapore uses Type G plugs at 230V, so Indian devices work but need a Type G adapter. UPI works only at select NETS merchants; carry some cash for hawker stalls and use a forex or low-markup card elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- NPCI International's partnership with NETS lets Indians pay at select Singapore merchants using UPI apps and RuPay cards ([NPCI International partnerships](https://npci.org.in/who-we-are/group-companies/npci-international/partnerships)).
- The PayNow-UPI linkage (launched 21 February 2023) is a person-to-person fund transfer system, not a merchant payment system ([PMO Singapore](https://www.pmo.gov.sg/newsroom/pm-lee-hsien-loong-at-the-launch-of-paynow-upi-linkage-feb-2023); [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=52223)).
- A 2023 report described Indian tourists paying via UPI by scanning a QR code ([Global Indian](https://globalindian.com/opinion/how-india-is-taking-upi-global)).

### Inferences
- UPI international must be activated in the user's UPI app and is accepted only where NETS displays UPI acceptance; do not rely on it at hawker stalls.
- Hawker stalls increasingly accept NETS and SGQR (PayNow) but these need a Singapore bank account; for visitors, cash (S$2, S$5, S$10 notes and coins) remains the safe option. Withdraw from bank ATMs (DBS, OCBC, UOB) and decline "dynamic currency conversion" (choose to be charged in SGD).

### Gaps
- Plug type and voltage (Type G, 230V, 50Hz) is standard but not sourced this session. Indian Type D or C plugs do not fit Type G sockets, so an adapter is needed; voltage matches India.
- Current UPI merchant coverage numbers in Singapore for 2026 were not found.
- ATM fees for Indian cards not researched.

## 12. Useful phrases

### Takeaway
Kopi and teh orders are built from a base drink plus modifiers; takeaway is "da bao" or "ta pau".

### Cited Findings
- Kopi: coffee with condensed milk; kopi-c: with evaporated milk and sugar; kopi-o: black with sugar; kopi-o kosong: black, no sugar ([Expat Living](https://expatliving.sg/coffee-fans-how-to-order-kopi-at-a-hawker-centre/); [Honeycombers](https://thehoneycombers.com/singapore/order-kopi-singapore/)).
- Modifiers: kosong (no sugar), siu dai (less sugar), ga dai (more sugar), po (weaker), gao (stronger), peng (iced), dai bao or da bao (takeaway) ([Expat Living](https://expatliving.sg/coffee-fans-how-to-order-kopi-at-a-hawker-centre/); [Naumi Hotels](https://naumihotels.com/singapore/blog-how-to-order-kopi-in-singapore/)).
- Order: choose the base (kopi or teh), then milk type (c for evaporated), then modifiers ([HoneyKids Asia](https://honeykidsasia.com/ordering-coffee-and-tea-like-a-local-in-singapore/)). Sources differ on whether siu dai means less sugar or less condensed milk ([Metro Residences](https://expat.metroresidences.com/sg/explore/coffee-you-meant-kopi-the-ultimate-singaporean-drink-guide-for-expats/)).

### Inferences
