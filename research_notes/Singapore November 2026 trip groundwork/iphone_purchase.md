Research date: Sunday 25 October 2026. Travellers: a couple on Indian passports, resident in India. Planned purchase: Tuesday 3 November at Apple Marina Bay Sands, with Apple Orchard Road (270 Orchard Road) on Thursday 5 November as the backup. Departure: Changi Terminal 3, early in the morning on Monday 9 November 2026 (a Singapore public holiday), landing in Mumbai later that morning.

## 2. Tourist GST refund (eTRS): rules, the realistic net refund, and claiming at Changi T3 on 9 November

### Takeaway
GST is 9 percent, so an S$1,899 iPhone contains S$156.80 of GST; after the handling fee the realistic net refund is about S$133 to S$142 (₹9,900 to ₹10,600), and the exact figure is printed on the eTRS ticket. Because the phone will be hand-carried, the claim is made at a kiosk in the T3 transit area after immigration, the box must be unused, and T3 refund counters are open 24 hours, so a 05:30 to 07:00 claim on the holiday morning is workable.

### Cited Findings
- Eligibility: a tourist aged 16 or over on the purchase date, not a Singapore citizen or permanent resident, not crew of the departing aircraft, and not a "Specified Person" (certain work, dependant, long-term visit and student pass holders) ([IRAS, Tourist Refund Scheme, last updated 31 March 2026](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme)).
- Minimum spend: at least S$100 including GST; up to three same-day invoices from retailers with the same GST registration number and shop name can be combined ([IRAS](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme); [Singapore Customs, Tourist Refund Scheme](https://www.customs.gov.sg/at-customs/departing-singapore/tourist-refund-scheme/)). Note that both sources say same-day receipts, so receipts from 3 November and 5 November cannot be combined.
- At purchase: the original passport must be shown in person (photocopies or images are not accepted), and the buyer should confirm receiving an original receipt and an eTRS transaction before leaving the shop ([IRAS](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme)).
- Participating retailers carry a "Global Blue" (Global Blue Singapore Pte Ltd) or "Global Tax Free" (Global Tax Free Pte Ltd) sign; participation is voluntary ([IRAS](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme)). Global Tax Free Pte Ltd is the approved central refund counter operator until 31 May 2027 and pays all refunds to tourists ([IRAS e-Tax Guide on eTRS, search summary](https://www.iras.gov.sg/media/docs/default-source/e-tax/gst-the-electronic-tourist-refund-scheme-(refund-claims-made-on-or-after-4-apr-2019).pdf?sfvrsn=e3f4d443_50); [Singapore Customs](https://www.customs.gov.sg/at-customs/departing-singapore/tourist-refund-scheme/)).
- Goods must be for personal use and "not consumed in Singapore, fully or partly", and must leave in the traveller's own luggage ([Singapore Customs](https://www.customs.gov.sg/at-customs/departing-singapore/tourist-refund-scheme/)). A travel blog (30 August 2026) states the product must be unopened and that an opened product's refund is denied ([Apple Price Hunt](https://applepricehunt.com/blog/buy-apple-products-singapore-changi-airport-layover)).
- Time limits: leave Singapore with the goods within two months of purchase; depart within 12 hours of the refund being approved; claim from the refund counter operator within two months of approval ([IRAS](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme); [Changi Airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/departing/immigration-and-customs.html)).
- Where to claim: goods to be checked in are processed at kiosks in the Departure Check-in Hall before check-in; hand-carried goods are processed at kiosks in the Departure Transit Lounge after immigration; the claim must be made at the departure terminal ([Singapore Customs](https://www.customs.gov.sg/at-customs/departing-singapore/tourist-refund-scheme/)). IRAS specifically routes "high-value or hand-carried goods" to the Departure Transit Lounge after immigration ([IRAS](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme)).
- Kiosk steps: choose language, scan the physical passport (transactions are retrieved automatically), accept conditions, select purchases, choose refund method ([Changi Airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/departing/immigration-and-customs.html); [Singapore Customs](https://www.customs.gov.sg/at-customs/departing-singapore/tourist-refund-scheme/)).
- Inspection: the kiosk may require the goods to be presented at the Customs Inspection Counter with the items, original receipts showing price, description and serial number, the physical passport, and the boarding pass or confirmed ticket ([Singapore Customs](https://www.customs.gov.sg/at-customs/departing-singapore/tourist-refund-scheme/)).
- T3 locations and hours: GST refund counters are open 24 hours daily; at T3 they are in the public departure hall between check-in rows 5 and 6, and in the transit area near the iShopChangi Collection Centre and Lotte Duty Free ([Changi Airport, Immigration and customs](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/departing/immigration-and-customs.html)).
- Refund methods at Changi: credit card (credited within 10 days), Alipay (immediate) or cash (collected with the passport at the GST Cash Refund counter in the Departure Transit Lounge after immigration) ([IRAS](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme); [Changi Airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/departing/immigration-and-customs.html)).
- Handling fee: IRAS says "The actual amount refunded to you will be less than the GST you have paid" because the retailer, central refund agency or counter operator deducts a handling fee; IRAS publishes no fixed amount for tourists ([IRAS](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme)). IRAS states that charges are shown on the eTRS ticket (search summary of the [IRAS e-Tax Guide](https://www.iras.gov.sg/media/docs/default-source/e-tax/gst-the-electronic-tourist-refund-scheme-(refund-claims-made-on-or-after-4-apr-2019).pdf?sfvrsn=e3f4d443_58)).
- Fee charged between operators: the IRAS e-Tax Guide sets a central clearing fee of S$2.20 per eTRS transaction plus 4.0 percent of the GST processed, effective 1 January 2024, billed to refund agencies and retailers rather than stated as a tourist deduction (search summary of the [IRAS e-Tax Guide](https://www.iras.gov.sg/media/docs/default-source/e-tax/gst-the-electronic-tourist-refund-scheme-(refund-claims-made-on-or-after-4-apr-2019).pdf?sfvrsn=e3f4d443_50)).
- Commercial guides estimate the tourist deduction at roughly 1.5 to 3 percent and the net refund at roughly 7 to 7.5 percent of the purchase price; these are unofficial and disagree ([Totally Singapore](https://totallysingapore.com/news-gst-refund-changes.html); [ScanSG](https://scansg.com/gst-refund.html); search summaries, not individually verified). A travel blog reports about 7 to 8 percent back, for example S$112 to S$128 on an S$1,599 MacBook Air ([Apple Price Hunt, 30 August 2026](https://applepricehunt.com/blog/buy-apple-products-singapore-changi-airport-layover)).
- Apple and eTRS: Apple's Singapore help text, quoted in a 2019 Apple Community thread, said GST refund "is not available for all purchases (including in-store pick-up orders) made at the Apple Online store" and that tourists can shop at an Apple Store for the refund if eligible ([Apple Community, August 2019](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/250542536)). Apple's current Singapore retail sales policy does not mention the Tourist Refund Scheme ([Apple Retail Sales Policy, Singapore](https://www.apple.com/legal/sales-support/sales-policies/retail_sg.html)).
- Removing goods from the check-in hall or transit area, or handing them to someone else after approval, is an offence, as are false declarations ([Singapore Customs](https://www.customs.gov.sg/at-customs/departing-singapore/tourist-refund-scheme/)).

### Inferences
- Gross GST in a GST-inclusive price is 9/109 of the price. My calculation of the realistic net refund, using 7.0 percent and 7.5 percent of the price as the bounds from the guides:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 Pro 256GB, S$1,899 | S$156.80 | S$132.93 | S$142.42 | ₹9,875 to ₹10,580 |
| 18 Pro Max 256GB, S$2,099 | S$173.31 | S$146.93 | S$157.42 | ₹10,915 to ₹11,694 |
| Duo 256GB, S$3,099 | S$255.88 | S$216.93 | S$232.42 | ₹16,115 to ₹17,266 |

- Apple store participation in eTRS is supported only by Apple's own wording quoted in 2019 and by general guides. Before paying on 3 November, ask the Specialist to confirm that an eTRS transaction will be issued for the in-store sale, and check that the receipt shows an eTRS ticket and the serial number. An online order for in-store pickup appears to be excluded in any case (see section 6).
- Use refund to credit card. Cash comes in Singapore dollars at the transit counter, which the couple would then have to spend at duty free or convert back to rupees at a poor rate. Alipay is not practical for Indian residents. A card refund lands in SGD and the bank converts it at its rate on the credit date, so it may differ slightly from the purchase-day rupee amount.
- The phone must stay sealed until the eTRS claim is approved in the transit area. Opening and setting it up in Singapore would breach the "not consumed in Singapore" condition and invite refusal at an inspection.
- Suggested 9 November sequence for the early-morning flight (T3): arrive at T3 by about 05:15 (the companion safety note recommends leaving Hillview by about 04:45); check in with the iPhone in the cabin bag; clear immigration; go straight to an eTRS kiosk in the transit area; if flagged, go to the Customs Inspection Counter with the sealed box, original receipt, passport and boarding pass; choose card refund; then go to the gate. Allow 20 to 30 minutes for the eTRS step in case of inspection. This is my planning estimate, not a published processing time.
- The public holiday should not matter for the refund counters because they run 24 hours daily.

### Gaps
- I found no official, current figure for the tourist-side handling fee, and no published tier table from Global Blue or Global Tax Free. The Global Blue Singapore page returned no body text when fetched. The exact deduction will only be visible on the eTRS ticket.
- I found no current (2025 or 2026) first-hand report confirming that Apple Marina Bay Sands or Apple Orchard Road issue eTRS tickets for iPhones. This is likely but should be confirmed in store.
- The opening hours and exact T3 location of the Customs Inspection Counter were not published on the pages I read. The location of the eTRS kiosks inside the T3 transit area was also not given specifically.

## 3. Indian customs at Mumbai T2 in November 2026: allowance, pooling, duty, red channel, penalties, personal effects, and a worked example

### Takeaway
The Baggage Rules, 2026 (in force since 2 February 2026) raised the duty-free allowance for an Indian resident arriving by air to ₹75,000 per person, it cannot be pooled between the two travellers, and a new sealed iPhone counts against it. The value above ₹75,000 attracts duty at an effective 35 percent according to the most specific source (38.5 percent according to another), and the phone must be declared in the red channel; on that basis a declared 256GB iPhone 18 Pro bought in Singapore lands about ₹8,600 to ₹15,000 cheaper than the Indian MRP, and the higher storage tiers of the Pro and Pro Max save little or nothing.

### Cited Findings
- The Union Budget 2026 announced higher duty-free baggage limits ([Business Standard, 1 February 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/budget/news/budget-2026-duty-free-baggage-limits-126020100715_1.html)). The government notified the Baggage Rules, 2026 by Notification No. 14/2026-Customs (N.T.) dated 1 February 2026, replacing the Baggage Rules, 2016 with effect from 2 February 2026, together with new Customs Baggage (Declaration and Processing) Regulations, 2026 and a Master Circular ([PIB release, via search summary; direct fetch returned HTTP 403](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2222384&reg=3&lang=1); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/cbic-notifies-baggage-rules-2026.html)).
- Allowance: ₹75,000 for residents, tourists of Indian origin and foreigners on non-tourist visas (not infants), up from ₹50,000; ₹25,000 for tourists of foreign origin; no general allowance for land-border arrivals ([Mumbai Customs Zone III, Arrival Passenger Guidelines, updated 23 October 2026](https://mumbaicustomszone3.gov.in/aarrival-passenger-guidelines); [PIB, via search summary](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2222384&reg=3&lang=1)).
- Separate laptop concession: one new laptop or notepad duty-free for passengers aged 18 or over, in addition to the allowance ([Mumbai Customs Zone III](https://mumbaicustomszone3.gov.in/aarrival-passenger-guidelines); [TaxSocial](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)).
- Pooling: free allowances cannot be combined with another passenger's allowance ([Mumbai Customs Zone III](https://mumbaicustomszone3.gov.in/aarrival-passenger-guidelines); [Bangalore Customs Public Notice 03/2026, via search summary](https://bangalorecustoms.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/bcc_pn_03_2026.pdf)). TaxSocial cites question 43 of the department's baggage FAQ to the same effect ([TaxSocial, 17 September 2026](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)).
- Phones: there is no separate phone allowance; a new phone counts against the ₹75,000; a sealed phone is treated as an article against the allowance; a used phone that qualifies as a personal effect under Rule 3 falls outside it, and there is no statutory one-phone rule, so the officer decides on the facts (SIM, personal data, no sealed box) ([TaxSocial](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)). Mumbai Customs confirms used personal effects are duty-free but does not mention phones ([Mumbai Customs Zone III](https://mumbaicustomszone3.gov.in/aarrival-passenger-guidelines)).
- Duty rate (most specific source): basic customs duty of 35 percent on the value above the allowance under Notification 26/2016-Customs as amended by Notification 4/2026-Customs; Social Welfare Surcharge nil for heading 9803 baggage since Notification 7/2025-Customs; IGST nil for baggage under Notification 2/2017-Integrated Tax (Rate), except vehicles, alcohol and tobacco; effective rate therefore 35 percent ([TaxSocial, 17 September 2026](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)).
- Conflicting rate: another secondary source in search results states 35 percent basic duty plus a 10 percent Social Welfare Surcharge on the duty, about 38.5 percent effective (search summary; attribution among [NRI Information](https://nriinformation.com/nri-info/nri-customs-duty), [FlightGPT](https://flightgpt.in/blog/indian-customs-duty-free-allowance-explained) and [Indian Eagle](https://www.indianeagle.com/travelbeats/new-customs-duty-and-duty-free-limit-for-carrying-foreign-items-to-india/) was unclear). I could not find Notification 7/2025-Customs itself to settle this.
- The widely reported cut from 20 percent to 10 percent applies to personal goods sent by post or courier under heading 9804 from 1 April 2026, not to passenger baggage under heading 9803 ([TaxSocial](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance); [CAalley](https://www.caalley.com/news-updates/budget-2026/50-cut-in-customs-duty-on-personal-goods-announced-in-budget-2026-check-how-much-you-will-likely-save)). Some travel blogs present a flat 10 percent baggage rate ([Indian Eagle](https://www.indianeagle.com/travelbeats/new-customs-duty-and-duty-free-limit-for-carrying-foreign-items-to-india/)); I treat that as a misreading.
- Valuation: customs starts from the declared transaction value, so carry the invoice; customs may redetermine a doubtful value; conversion uses the CBIC-notified exchange rate for the declaration date ([TaxSocial](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)).
- Declaration: passengers with goods above the allowance must use the red channel and file a Customs Declaration Form; pre-declaration is possible through the ATITHI app ([Mumbai Customs Zone III](https://mumbaicustomszone3.gov.in/aarrival-passenger-guidelines)). TaxSocial says Form CBD-I can be filed on arrival or up to three days before through ATITHI or ICEGATE, and duty is paid at the counter, including by card, with a receipt issued ([TaxSocial](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)).
- Penalties: using the green channel while carrying dutiable goods can lead to prosecution, penalties and confiscation ([Mumbai Customs Zone III](https://mumbaicustomszone3.gov.in/aarrival-passenger-guidelines)). Undeclared goods are liable to confiscation under section 111(l) of the Customs Act, 1962, misdeclared goods under section 111(m), with a penalty under section 112, a redemption fine under section 125 on release, and duty on top; a passenger who does not want to pay can have the article detained against a receipt and take it out on departure ([TaxSocial](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)).
- Anecdotal (undated): a traveller arriving at Mumbai from Dubai with a sealed iPhone 17 Pro Max was stopped at the scanner and required to declare it at the red channel and pay duty on the value above the allowance ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/your-money/saving-investment/he-flew-from-dubai-with-new-iphone-17-indian-customs-stops-him-charges-duty-1.500407474)). An older Taxscan report describes three Mumbai airport customs officials suspended for threatening a passenger carrying an iPhone ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/?p=255428)).
- TaxSocial's own worked example (iPhone 18 Pro 256GB, declared, only dutiable item): bought in Dubai at ₹1,32,982 the duty is ₹20,294 for a total of ₹1,53,276; bought in the US at ₹1,14,774 the duty is ₹13,921 for ₹1,28,695; both against the Indian MRP of ₹1,64,900 ([TaxSocial](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)).

### Inferences
Worked example: one traveller buys one sealed iPhone 18 Pro 256GB at Apple Marina Bay Sands on 3 November for S$1,899, claims eTRS at Changi, and declares the phone at Mumbai. The phone is that traveller's only dutiable item. Assumptions: ₹74.2862 per S$1; customs value equals the invoice price including Singapore GST (the cautious case); duty 35 percent; net eTRS refund 7.25 percent of the price (midpoint).

| Step | Amount |
|---|---|
| Price paid, S$1,899 at ₹74.2862 | ₹1,41,069 |
| Less net eTRS refund, about S$137.68 | minus ₹10,228 |
| Customs value | ₹1,41,069 |
| Less resident allowance | minus ₹75,000 |
| Value liable to duty | ₹66,069 |
| Duty at 35% | plus ₹23,124 |
| Landed cost with a zero-markup card | ₹1,53,966 |
| Apple India MRP | ₹1,64,900 |

## 4. Compatibility for use in India: SIM, bands, shutter sound, model numbers

### Takeaway
The Singapore iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max are the same models Apple sells in India (A3714 and A3717), with a nano-SIM tray plus eSIM, so bands and behaviour match an Indian-bought phone. Avoid US, Japanese and Gulf units, which are eSIM-only or carry other restrictions.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's Singapore cellular page lists the Singapore iPhone 18 Pro as A3714 and the Pro Max as A3717, and gives India the same model numbers; the US uses A3472 and A3473, Japan A3713 and A3716, and mainland China A3715 and A3718 ([Apple SG, iPhone cellular and wireless](https://www.apple.com/sg/iphone/cellular/)).
- The Singapore variant's 5G NR bands include n1, n3, n5, n8, n28, n40, n41, n77 and n78, and its LTE bands include FDD bands 3, 5 and 8 and TD-LTE bands 40 and 41; mmWave n258 is listed only for the US models ([Apple SG, iPhone cellular and wireless](https://www.apple.com/sg/iphone/cellular/)).
- The iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max are eSIM-only in 12 markets (US, Canada, Mexico, Japan, Guam, US Virgin Islands, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE); units sold in Singapore and India have a nano-SIM tray plus eSIM ([iThinkDifferent](https://www.ithinkdiff.com/iphone-18-pro-esim-only-countries/); [MobiMatter](https://mobimatter.com/blog/iphone-18-and-iphone-duo-esim-only-or-dual-sim/); Apple India support page via search summary: [Apple Support IN](https://support.apple.com/en-in/118569)).
- One source lists A3714 as the model for "190 other countries and regions" and A3713 for Canada, Japan, Mexico and the Gulf states ([Code eSIM](https://codeesim.com/blog/iphone-18-pro-model-numbers-by-country/)). This conflicts in part with Apple's page, which assigns A3713 to Japan; Apple's page is the primary source.
- iPhones sold in Japan and South Korea always play the shutter sound, and the behaviour appears tied to the SIM in use ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3610749/how-to-disable-the-camera-shutter-sound-on-an-iphone-or-ipad.html)).

### Inferences
- Since the Singapore and Indian units share a model number, the Singapore phone supports exactly the bands an Indian-market iPhone 18 Pro supports, and it will work on Jio, Airtel and Vi in the same way.
- The shutter sound should behave as on an Indian phone (silenced with the Silent mode switch or Action button), because neither Singapore nor India is a forced-shutter market. This follows from the shared model number; I did not find a source that states it for these two countries.
- A sealed Singapore unit carries no carrier lock when bought from Apple; Apple's buy pages say iPhones bought from apple.com without a carrier are unlocked ([Apple SG, Buy iPhone 18 Pro](https://www.apple.com/sg/shop/buy-iphone/iphone-18-pro)).

## 5. Warranty and AppleCare+

### Takeaway
Apple's warranty terms allow it to restrict iPhone warranty service to the country of original sale, and reports on Apple India honouring Singapore-bought iPhones are mixed and mostly old. The shared A3714 model number removes the parts-compatibility excuse but not the policy risk; AppleCare+ bought in Singapore is not designed for an Indian resident and its use in India is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's one-year limited warranty text for hardware states that Apple "may restrict warranty service for hardware products to the country where Apple or its Authorized Distributors originally sold the device" (Australia and Canada versions) ([Apple AU warranty](https://www.apple.com/au/legal/warranty/products/accessory-warranty-english.html); [Apple CA warranty](https://www.apple.com/ca/legal/warranty/products/accessory-warranty-english.html)). Forum posts quote iPhone-specific wording naming iPhone, iPad, Apple TV and HomePod (search summary of [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255236973)).
- A 2017 GSMArena report said Apple India would honour warranties on iPhones bought outside India ([GSMArena, 2017](https://www.gsmarena.com/newscomm-27920.php)). A more recent forum post says Indian service centres usually refer hardware defects back to the country of purchase while handling software issues regardless (search summary; [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255236973)). These are conflicting and largely anecdotal.
- AppleCare+: Apple's community team says AppleCare+ must be bought in the country of residence because terms follow local regulation, while it does cover use abroad when travelling (search summary of [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253503511)). Singapore AppleCare+ accidental damage cover is provided under a group insurance policy (search summary of Apple SG AppleCare pages). An older TechCrunch list of countries where AppleCare+ covered travellers' iPhones included Singapore but not India ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=883117)).
- Apple Singapore returns: 14 days from receipt, original condition, and returns only in the country of purchase ([Apple Retail Sales Policy, Singapore](https://www.apple.com/legal/sales-support/sales-policies/retail_sg.html)).

### Inferences
- The practical risk is a hardware fault in months 2 to 12. Out-of-box faults are best caught in Singapore: the couple could open and test the phone in the store on 3 November, but that conflicts with the eTRS requirement that goods not be used in Singapore. They need to choose between in-store testing and a clean eTRS claim. Leaving the box sealed and testing at home is the usual choice, accepting a small warranty risk.
- I would not buy Singapore AppleCare+ for a phone that will live in India. Whether AppleCare+ India can be added to a Singapore-bought phone is unknown (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I did not read Apple's current Singapore iPhone warranty text or the Singapore AppleCare+ terms directly.
- I found no 2025 or 2026 report from an Indian owner of a Singapore-bought iPhone getting a hardware warranty repair in India.
- Whether AppleCare+ can be bought in India for a device purchased abroad was not established.

## 7. Payment: card choice, forex markup, TCS and dynamic currency conversion

### Takeaway
Pay in Singapore dollars with a zero-markup Visa or Mastercard credit card in the buyer's name; a typical 3.5 percent markup plus 18 percent GST adds about 4.13 percent (₹5,800 on a 256GB Pro), which halves the saving. Overseas credit card spend is not subject to TCS in 2026 according to current reporting, and decline any offer to be charged in rupees.

### Cited Findings
- Indian card forex markups typically run 1 to 3.5 percent, with 18 percent GST charged on the markup; a ₹1 lakh spend at 3.5 percent costs ₹4,130, or 4.13 percent effective; RBI requires disclosure of the markup but does not cap it ([CardTrail](https://cardtrail.in/india-rules/forex-markup-charges-india-credit-cards/)).
- Zero-markup options cited in the companion budget note: Niyo Global (SBM Bank, prepaid Visa) and Scapia (Federal Bank) credit card; some cards marketed as zero-markup charge 1.5 to 3.5 percent ([Jupiter](https://jupiter.money/blog/zero-forex-markup-credit-card/)).
- Dynamic currency conversion (paying in INR at a foreign terminal) adds a markup estimated at 2 to 4 percent ([AU Small Finance Bank](https://www.au.bank.in/blogs/what-is-dynamic-currency-conversion)), 3 to 5 percent ([IndusInd Bank](https://www.indusind.bank.in/iblogs/credit-card/dynamic-currency-conversion-dcc-understanding-it-and-markup-fees/)) or 3 to 7 percent ([HappyFares](https://happyfares.in/blog/dcc-pay-in-inr-or-local-currency-flights-2026/)), on top of the card's own markup; sources advise paying in local currency.
- TCS on credit card spend abroad: the 2023 plan to bring card spends under LRS for TCS was deferred indefinitely in September 2023, and card spend abroad is not currently subject to TCS ([InvestingPro](https://www.investingpro.in/articles/tcs-credit-card-foreign-spend-india-2026-deferral-status); [BookMyForex](https://www.bookmyforex.com/blog/new-tcs-rate-on-foreign-remittances/); search summaries).
- TCS under LRS more generally: the threshold is ₹10 lakh per financial year from 1 April 2025; forex card loads and LRS remittances for other purposes generally carry no TCS up to ₹10 lakh and 20 percent above it, per one source; Budget 2026 set a flat 2 percent on overseas tour packages from 1 April 2026 ([BookMyForex](https://www.bookmyforex.com/blog/new-tcs-rate-on-foreign-remittances/); [HappyFares News](https://www.happyfares.in/news/tcs-foreign-travel-india-2026); [Dinesh Aarjav](https://www.dineshaarjav.com/blog-detail/tcs-changes-in-budget-2026-flat-2-percent)). Sources disagree on some rates.

### Inferences
- A ₹1.41 lakh purchase is far below the ₹10 lakh LRS threshold, so even on a forex card no TCS should arise unless the buyer has already used most of the threshold this financial year. On a credit card, TCS does not apply on current reporting.
- Use a credit card for the purchase rather than a prepaid forex card, so the eTRS card refund has a clear destination and any dispute is easier. A zero-markup credit card is ideal; failing that, a zero-markup prepaid card loaded in SGD avoids conversion at the till.
- Check the card's per-transaction and international limits before travel; an S$1,899 to S$3,099 charge may exceed a default international limit or trigger a fraud block. Enable international use and keep the bank's helpline handy.
- If the Apple terminal or receipt offers an INR amount, choose SGD.

### Gaps
- Whether Apple's Singapore terminals offer dynamic currency conversion at all was not established.
- RuPay acceptance at Apple Singapore stores was not established.
- I did not read a CBDT or RBI primary source on the TCS deferral for credit cards; the finding rests on secondary financial sites.

## 9. Decision summary and step-by-step plan

### Takeaway
Buy only if the model is a 256GB iPhone 18 Pro (saving about ₹8,600 to ₹15,000 after duty with a zero-markup card), a 512GB Pro (about ₹4,000 to ₹7,500), or a Duo if one can be found; the 1TB and 2TB Pro and the 512GB and larger Pro Max save little or cost more than buying in India once duty is paid. If the main goal is a 256GB Pro Max, the saving is about ₹4,000 to ₹7,000 and falls to near zero on a marked-up card.

### Cited Findings
- All facts used in this plan are cited in sections 1 to 8: Apple SG and IN prices ([Apple SG](https://www.apple.com/sg/shop/buy-iphone/iphone-18-pro); [Apple IN](https://www.apple.com/in/shop/buy-iphone/iphone-18-pro)), eTRS rules ([IRAS](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-(gst)/consumers/tourist-refund-scheme); [Singapore Customs](https://www.customs.gov.sg/at-customs/departing-singapore/tourist-refund-scheme/); [Changi Airport](https://www.changiairport.com/en/airport-guide/departing/immigration-and-customs.html)), Indian baggage rules ([Mumbai Customs Zone III](https://mumbaicustomszone3.gov.in/aarrival-passenger-guidelines); [TaxSocial](https://taxsocial.pro/article/iphone-18-pro-iphone-17-from-dubai-usa-to-india-customs-duty-baggage-rules-2026-rs-75000-allowance)), model compatibility ([Apple SG cellular](https://www.apple.com/sg/iphone/cellular/)) and iShopChangi ([iShopChangi](https://www.ishopchangi.com/en/product/apple-iphone-18-pro-mp00941668)).

### Inferences
Steps, in order:

1. Before leaving India (by 1 November): confirm a zero-markup Visa or Mastercard credit card in the buyer's name, raise its international and per-transaction limit to cover at least S$2,200 (or S$3,200 for a Duo), and tell the bank about the trip. Decide which traveller buys and carries the phone; that person should keep other shopping light. Press "Notify Me" on iShopChangi for the chosen model.
2. On 2 to 3 November: check apple.com/sg for in-store availability of the exact configuration at Marina Bay Sands and Orchard Road.
3. Tuesday 3 November, Apple Marina Bay Sands (10:00 to 22:00 in late October; confirm November hours): the buyer brings the original passport. Before paying, ask the Specialist to confirm an eTRS transaction will be issued. Pay in SGD, decline any INR offer. Check that the receipt shows the serial number and an eTRS ticket. Keep the box sealed and keep the receipt with the passport.
4. Thursday 5 November, Apple Orchard Road: use only if the 3 November purchase failed. A purchase on a different day needs its own S$100 minimum, which any iPhone meets.
5. By the evening of Saturday 7 November: if iShopChangi has the model back in stock and its Collection Centre hours and stock origin check out, ordering there instead of Apple saves a further S$14 to S$24 against the net eTRS refund.
6. Monday 9 November, Changi T3: arrive by about 05:15 with the phone in the cabin bag. Check in, clear immigration, then use an eTRS kiosk in the transit area. If flagged, present the sealed phone, receipt, passport and boarding pass at the Customs Inspection Counter. Choose refund to credit card (credited within 10 days). Do not open the phone in Singapore.
7. Before landing (within three days of arrival, or on arrival): the carrier files the customs declaration on the ATITHI app, listing the phone at its invoice value.
8. Mumbai T2: take the red channel, show the invoice and phone, and pay duty at 35 percent on the value above ₹75,000 (about ₹23,100 for a 256GB Pro at the GST-inclusive value). Pay by card and keep the receipt with the Apple invoice for warranty and resale.
9. At home: set the phone up and test it at once. For a hardware fault, contact Apple India support with the serial number; be ready for the possibility that Apple India refers a warranty repair back to Singapore.

### Gaps
- The plan rests on three unverified points that can change the numbers: the duty rate (35 percent versus 38.5 percent), the tourist-side eTRS handling fee, and Apple Singapore stores' current eTRS participation. The first can be checked by calling Mumbai Customs or reading Notification 7/2025-Customs; the second and third can be confirmed at the Apple store before paying.
