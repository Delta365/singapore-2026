# Singapore conditions and events, 2 to 9 November 2026

Research date: 24 October 2026. Everything below is anchored to the eight-day window 2 to 9 November 2026.

A note on the two most important findings up front, because they change the picture against the usual guidebook answer:
1. Singapore is in **strong El Nino conditions** that are forecast to peak during October to December 2026, with a **positive Indian Ocean Dipole expected to persist through November 2026**. Both push towards drier and hotter than the long-run November average.
2. **Transboundary haze has been an active problem through September and October 2026**, with 24-hour PSI readings in the unhealthy to very unhealthy range in central Singapore. This is a live operational risk for early November, not a theoretical one.

## Weather in Singapore in early November: climatology

### Takeaway
On the long-run average November is one of Singapore's wetter and least sunny months: roughly 252 mm of rain across about 19 rain days, highs near 31 C, lows near 25 C, humidity near 85%, and the highest lightning-day count of the year. Critically, **early November is still the inter-monsoon period, not the Northeast Monsoon**, which begins in December.

### Cited Findings
All figures below are the 1991 to 2020 climatological means for Changi Climate Station, published by the Meteorological Service Singapore:
- November mean monthly rainfall total: **252.3 mm**, across about **19 rain days** — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)
- November mean daily maximum temperature **31.2 C**; mean daily minimum **24.6 C**; 24-hour mean **27.2 C** — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)
- November 24-hour mean relative humidity: **84.9%** — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)
- November mean wind speed **1.4 m/s**, the lowest of any month of the year — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)
- November has **19 thunderstorm days and 22 lightning days**, the latter the highest of the year — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)
- November sunshine is **among the lowest of the year, alongside December** — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)
- **November to January is the higher-rainfall stretch of the year**, when the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone lies near Singapore — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)

### Monsoon definitions and what they mean for early November
- The **Northeast Monsoon runs from December to early March**, with northerly to northeasterly winds. Its wet phase, bringing widespread continuous moderate to heavy rain during monsoon surges, is usually December to early January. Its dry, windy phase is usually late January to early March. January and February are the windiest months — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)
- **MSS does not publish a fixed Northeast Monsoon onset date.** The page defines the season by month range only — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)
- **October to November is the second inter-monsoon period**, characterised by light and variable winds, with afternoon and early-evening thunderstorms that are at times severe. This period is generally wetter than the March-to-May inter-monsoon — [MSS, Climate of Singapore](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/)

### Inferences
- The 2 to 9 November window sits squarely in the second inter-monsoon period. The practical consequence is a **convective, afternoon-driven rainfall pattern**: hot, humid, bright mornings, with heavy but relatively short-lived thunderstorms most likely in the afternoon and early evening. This is different in character from the Northeast Monsoon's all-day rain, which a visitor will not see in early November.
- Mornings are the reliable outdoor window. Afternoon activity should have an indoor fallback.
- Light winds (1.4 m/s, the year's minimum) mean the humidity feels heavier than the temperature suggests, and it also means any haze that arrives tends to sit over the island rather than clear quickly.
- 252.3 mm over 19 rain days averages roughly 13 mm per rain day, so "19 rain days" describes frequent brief downpours rather than sustained all-day rain.
- Because November is one of the wetter months in the long run, a visitor who reads only climate averages would over-prepare for rain in this particular year. See the forecast section below.

### Gaps
- **I could not find an explicit figure for mean monthly sunshine hours in November.** The MSS climatology page I reached describes November sunshine qualitatively as among the lowest of the year, alongside December, but did not give a number of hours in the extract retrieved. The MSS historical daily records pages would hold this.

## MSS outlook for late 2026: El Nino or La Nina signal, monsoon onset, haze risk

### Takeaway
MSS classifies the current state as **El Nino conditions**, strong by the index, forecast to peak during October to December 2026, with a positive Indian Ocean Dipole expected to persist through November 2026. The combination points to drier and hotter than average. Haze is not a hypothetical risk in November 2026: it has already produced unhealthy air repeatedly in September and October.

### Cited Findings: ENSO and IOD status
- MSS's El Nino / La Nina status page, **updated 16 October 2026**, classifies the state as **"El Nino Conditions"** — [MSS, El Nino / La Nina Status](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-el-la/)
- Latest **Relative Nino3.4 index: 2.1 C for September 2026**; the three-month July to September 2026 average was **1.7 C** — [MSS, El Nino / La Nina Status](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-el-la/)
- El Nino is **expected to continue into early 2027**, with the index **predicted to peak during October to December 2026**. Most models expect weakening in January to February 2027; more than 90% still show El Nino present in March 2027 — [MSS, El Nino / La Nina Status](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-el-la/)
- **Positive Indian Ocean Dipole conditions were present in September 2026 and are expected to persist through November 2026**, returning to neutral by January 2027 — [MSS, El Nino / La Nina Status](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-el-la/)
- La Nina was the preceding state: the index passed the La Nina threshold in September 2025 and returned to neutral in March 2026 — [MSS, El Nino / La Nina Status](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-el-la/)
- ENSO has its strongest correlation with Singapore rainfall during the Southwest Monsoon (June to September); monthly variability remains considerable even in moderate or strong events — [MSS, El Nino / La Nina Status](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-el-la/)

### Cited Findings: the May 2026 NEA/MSS seasonal release
- Release **dated 29 May 2026**: MSS expected El Nino conditions to develop in June or July 2026 with **more than an 80% chance**, initially moderate with possible strengthening in August or September — [NEA/MSS news release, 29 May 2026](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/el-ni%C3%B1o-conditions-expected-in-second-half-of-2026--with-warmer-and-drier-weather-in-the-coming-months)
- Same release: a **positive IOD may develop in July or August 2026, also bringing drier conditions** to the region — [NEA/MSS, 29 May 2026](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/el-ni%C3%B1o-conditions-expected-in-second-half-of-2026--with-warmer-and-drier-weather-in-the-coming-months)
- Singapore expected to be **drier, especially during the Southwest Monsoon**. For calibration, during the last strong El Nino in 2015, June to September rainfall was **about 35% below the long-term average** — [NEA/MSS, 29 May 2026](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/el-ni%C3%B1o-conditions-expected-in-second-half-of-2026--with-warmer-and-drier-weather-in-the-coming-months)
- Temperatures expected to rise, with the **greatest impact in March to May 2027** as the event weakens. In the 2016 decay period, March to May temperatures were 1.0 C above average — [NEA/MSS, 29 May 2026](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/el-ni%C3%B1o-conditions-expected-in-second-half-of-2026--with-warmer-and-drier-weather-in-the-coming-months)
- Haze risk: rises from June 2026 as hot, dry conditions favour peat and vegetation fires regionally. The release said the **dry season could be more intense and prolonged, possibly extending into October** — [NEA/MSS, 29 May 2026](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/el-ni%C3%B1o-conditions-expected-in-second-half-of-2026--with-warmer-and-drier-weather-in-the-coming-months)
- **The 29 May 2026 release does not mention the Northeast Monsoon or November.** Its haze and dry-period outlook ends at October — [NEA/MSS, 29 May 2026](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/el-ni%C3%B1o-conditions-expected-in-second-half-of-2026--with-warmer-and-drier-weather-in-the-coming-months)

### Inferences
- **Separate the climatology from the forecast.** Long-run November climatology says wet. The actual 2026 signal (strong El Nino peaking Oct to Dec, positive IOD through November) says drier and hotter than the 252 mm / 19-rain-day average. A visitor should plan for heat and sun first, rain second, while still carrying an umbrella because inter-monsoon thunderstorms remain possible on any afternoon.
- **Transboundary haze is the single most likely thing to disrupt this trip.** Readings of 180 to 225 PSI occurred within three weeks of the window. Outdoor-heavy days (Sentosa, Southern Ridges, Gardens by the Bay outdoor gardens, East Coast Park, Pulau Ubin) should be treated as flexible and swapped for indoor alternatives if PSI is elevated. Carrying N95 masks is a reasonable precaution for this specific year, which would not be normal advice for a typical November.
- Because the Northeast Monsoon starts in December, no monsoon-surge rain should be expected during 2 to 9 November. The wind regime is light and variable, which is also why haze lingers.
- The absence of any November 2026 fortnightly outlook at the time of research is expected rather than suspicious: MSS issues fortnightly outlooks roughly on the 1st and 16th, so **the outlook covering 1 to 15 November 2026 would be published around 31 October or 1 November 2026**, within a week or two of the window. That is the single highest-value thing to check before departure.

### Inferences
- **The visitor's practical read:** the Serangoon Road lights will be up and lit nightly 7pm to midnight across the entire 2 to 9 November window, on every available reading of the sources. The Campbell Lane Festival Village will be running for the first six evenings of the window and will be in its final days, which is historically when it is busiest.
- **Peak Little India crowding should be expected on the evenings of Friday 6 and Saturday 7 November**, the last weekend before Deepavali, when last-minute shopping for garlands, sweets and new clothes collides with the bazaar's closing days and the three-day long weekend. Saturday 7 November evening is likely the single most congested time in Little India during the window. Tekka Centre and the Mustafa Centre area typically absorb much of this.
- The ambiguity in **"closes on Deepavali itself"** matters. It could mean the Village's final trading day is 8 November, or that the Village is shut on 8 November after trading through 7 November. The stated run "10 October to 8 November" favours 8 November being the final day. **Treat 8 November as the last day but not guaranteed to be trading normally**, and verify with LISHA.
- **On Deepavali day (Sunday 8 November) the character of Little India shifts** from commercial bustle to religious observance: temple visits in the morning (Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple on Serangoon Road, Sri Srinivasa Perumal Temple), family gatherings, and many shops and eateries operating reduced hours or closing. A visitor wanting the bazaar should go before 8 November. A visitor wanting the religious and cultural side should go on 8 November, early, and dress respectfully.
- **Monday 9 November is a public holiday with a quieter Little India** but busy malls, attractions and leisure venues islandwide.
- The one source placing Deepavali on 7 November is an outlier against the MOM-derived consensus and should be disregarded for planning, though it usefully flags that **Naraka Chaturdashi observance on the eve, 7 November, is itself part of the festival** for some communities, reinforcing the expectation of heavy activity that Saturday.

### Inferences
- The list above counts to exactly 11 gazetted days, which matches the reported total and is a useful internal consistency check.
- **What to expect on Monday 9 November specifically:**

  - Public transport runs on a Sunday or public-holiday timetable, meaning slightly reduced frequency and sometimes a later start.

- The three-day weekend of 7 to 9 November also means **domestic leisure demand peaks**: hotel rates in Singapore, restaurant reservations and Sentosa or Universal Studios tickets are harder to get on those three days. It is also a popular short-haul outbound travel weekend for residents, so Changi will be busy on Friday 6 and Saturday 7 November for departures and on Monday 9 November for returns.

## Crowd patterns and avoidance tactics

### Takeaway
I could not find measured crowd data for Singapore attractions, so this section is largely inference from the structure of this particular window rather than sourced fact. The structural read is clear: Monday 2 to Friday 6 November are ordinary working days and the quietest of the eight; Saturday 7 to Monday 9 November is a three-day public-holiday weekend and will be the busiest stretch.

### Cited Findings
- The 7 to 9 November long weekend is one of six long weekends in 2026, up from four in 2025 — [Malay Mail, 16 June 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2025/06/16/mark-your-calendars-singaporeans-get-six-long-weekends-in-2026-for-travel-sleep-ins-or-just-escaping-the-boss/180547)
- Singapore school November/December holidays do **not** begin until 22 November 2026, so the window falls during school term — [Naumi Hotels listing](https://www.naumihotels.com/naumi-hotel-singapore/events-meetings/upcoming-events-in-singapore)
- The Little India Festival Village runs nightly from 7pm and the street lights are on 7pm to midnight, concentrating Little India footfall into the evening — [Little Big Red Dot, 2 October 2026](https://littlebigreddot.com/?p=27487)

### Inferences
- **Quietest days in the window: Tuesday 3, Wednesday 4 and Thursday 5 November.** Monday 2 November is also quiet but loses several museums to Monday closures. The window falling in school term rather than the school holidays is a meaningful advantage: local family demand at Sentosa, the Mandai attractions and Jewel is materially lower in term time.
- **Busiest: Saturday 7, Sunday 8 and Monday 9 November**, in that rough order for general attractions. Sentosa, Universal Studios, the Mandai wildlife parks, Jewel Changi and the major malls should be expected to be at long-weekend levels on all three days.
- **Little India is the inverse case**: it peaks on the evenings of Friday 6 and Saturday 7 November, as the bazaar's closing days coincide with pre-Deepavali shopping, then shifts on Sunday 8 November to temple-centred observance with many shops shut. Monday 9 November in Little India is likely the calmest of the three.
- Practical tactics that follow from the structure:
  - Do the big paid attractions on Tuesday to Thursday and leave Saturday to Monday for neighbourhoods, hawker centres and indoor venues that absorb crowds better.
  - **Go early.** Attractions that open at 9am or 10am are quietest in the first ninety minutes, which in this window also coincides with the only reliably rain-free and lowest-UV part of the day.
  - For Little India, visit the bazaar on a weekday evening (Tuesday 3 to Thursday 5 November) rather than the final weekend, and visit in daylight if the aim is photography of the shophouses rather than the lights.
  - Book timed-entry attractions and restaurant tables for 7 to 9 November well ahead, and expect public-holiday pricing on 9 November.
  - Avoid Changi Airport and the MRT lines to it on the evening of Friday 6 November and the morning of Saturday 7 November (outbound long-weekend rush) and on Monday 9 November (returns).
  - Hawker centres peak at local lunch hours, roughly 12pm to 1.30pm on working days. Going at 11.30am or after 2pm cuts queueing substantially.

### Gaps
- **I found no sourced, quantitative crowd or footfall data** for Singapore attractions by day of week or time of day: no LTA ridership breakdowns, no Sentosa or Universal Studios attendance figures, no Google-popular-times aggregates. Everything in the Inferences above is reasoning from the holiday calendar and the published event schedule, and should be presented to the reader as such rather than as measurement.
- **No published guidance from any attraction on expected peak days during the Deepavali long weekend 2026.**

## Practical seasonal notes: dengue, sun, clothing, air conditioning, religious observances

### Takeaway
Dengue activity is low by Singapore's recent standards but not negligible, with three red-alert clusters active in mid-October. The dominant seasonal risks for this window are heat, high UV and haze rather than rain. Ramadan does not fall anywhere near the window; the religious observance that matters is Deepavali itself.

### Cited Findings: dengue
- **79 dengue cases in the week ending 10 October 2026**, eight more than the 71 recorded in the week ending 3 October — [Little Big Red Dot](https://littlebigreddot.com/?p=28887)
- **2,643 cases in the first 40 weeks of 2026** — [Little Big Red Dot](https://littlebigreddot.com/?p=28887)
- **Seven active clusters as of Friday 16 October 2026, three at red-alert level** (10 or more cases): **Lentor Gardens, Bishan Street 12, and the Ho Ching Road area**. The largest cluster spans Ho Ching Road, Kang Ching Road, Tah Ching Road and Yuan Ching Road, with **95 cases since inception**, four of them in the preceding two weeks — [Little Big Red Dot](https://littlebigreddot.com/?p=28887)

- Singapore recorded its **2026 weekly high in late May 2026**, as the city-state entered the peak mosquito season — [Malay Mail, 27 May 2026](https://www.malaymail.com/amp/news/singapore/2026/05/27/singapore-dengue-cases-hit-2026-high-as-city-state-enters-peak-mosquito-season/221537)

### Cited Findings: heat, sun and air quality
- MSS outlook for the first fortnight of October 2026: **daily maximum temperatures likely 34 C to 35 C on most days** — [NST / Bernama, 2 October 2026](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/world/region/2026/10/1545944/singapore-face-hot-weather-thunderstorms-early-october)

### Cited Findings: religious observances in or near the window
- **Hari Raya Puasa 2026 fell on Saturday 21 March 2026**, placing Ramadan in roughly February to March 2026 — [Malay Mail, 16 June 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2025/06/16/mark-your-calendars-singaporeans-get-six-long-weekends-in-2026-for-travel-sleep-ins-or-just-escaping-the-boss/180547)
- **Deepavali is Sunday 8 November 2026**, with the religious observance centred on Hindu temples and homes — [Little Big Red Dot, 2 October 2026](https://littlebigreddot.com/?p=27487)

### Inferences
- **Ramadan is not a factor.** With Hari Raya Puasa on 21 March 2026, Ramadan ran roughly mid-February to mid-March 2026 and is seven months behind the window. Kampong Glam, Geylang Serai and Malay-Muslim eateries will be on normal hours.
- **Dengue precautions are proportionate rather than urgent.** Weekly case counts in the high 70s are low for Singapore, and the active red clusters are in residential areas (Lentor, Bishan, Jurong West / Ho Ching Road) that are mostly off a visitor's itinerary. Standard repellent in the evening, especially around parks, nature reserves and greenery-heavy hotel grounds, is sufficient. Check the NEA dengue cluster map against the accommodation address before booking if the stay is in a residential area.
- **Heat and UV are the bigger practical concern this year than rain.** With El Nino conditions, a positive IOD through November, and October highs of 34 C to 35 C, plan for serious heat: outdoor activity before 11am and after 5pm, high-SPF sunscreen reapplied, a hat, and a litre or more of water per outdoor half-day. Singapore sits one degree north of the equator, so the UV index routinely reaches extreme levels around midday even under hazy or partly cloudy skies.
- **Clothing:** lightweight, breathable, loose-fitting natural fibres; sandals or shoes that tolerate a sudden downpour; a compact umbrella or packable rain shell; and one light long-sleeved layer or wrap for indoor air conditioning, which in Singapore malls, cinemas, taxis and offices is routinely set cold enough to be uncomfortable after a sweaty walk. For temple visits in Little India around Deepavali, shoulders and knees covered, and shoes removed at the entrance.
- **Haze contingency:** given the September and October 2026 record, pack N95 or KN95 masks and plan at least two fully indoor alternative days (National Gallery, National Museum, ArtScience Museum, Jewel, the conservatories, a hawker-centre and mall day). Check the NEA PSI readings and the myENV app each morning.
- **Lightning is a real operational constraint in November**, not a footnote. With 22 lightning days a month on average, outdoor pools, Sentosa beaches, golf courses, water parks and some outdoor attractions suspend operations during lightning risk alerts. Expect intermittent afternoon closures of outdoor facilities.
- **Hawker and shop hours around Deepavali:** many Indian-run stalls and shops in Little India and Tekka Centre close or shorten hours on 8 and 9 November. Anyone planning an Indian-food day should do it earlier in the window.

The authoritative current data is on the NEA dengue pages and the data.gov.sg cluster dataset.
