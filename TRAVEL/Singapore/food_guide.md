# Food guide

Food is the largest part of this trip and more than half of the expected spend. This guide covers the dishes, the stalls and restaurants in the agreed plan, how hawker centres work, dietary points, and where every item from your wish list and saved videos ended up.

Prices are in S$ with INR at S$1 = ₹74.10. Many stall prices come from older reviews and are dated where the notes give a date; expect 2026 prices to be a little higher. Opening days from food blogs change from year to year, so check the riskiest ones (marked in the table) before you go.

## Dining etiquette

### Tray return

Return your tray, crockery and litter (tissues, wet wipes, straws, wrappers and cans) to the tray-return racks. Leaving them on the table counts as littering under the Environmental Public Health Act, enforced at hawker centres since 1 September 2021 and at coffee shops and food courts since 1 January 2022. First offenders who ignore an officer's advice get a written warning; repeat offenders face fines (S$300 has been reported) or court. Many centres have separate racks for halal and non-halal crockery, so use the one marked for your stall.

### Chope

A packet of tissues on a seat or table means someone has "choped" (reserved) it, so sit elsewhere. You can do the same while one of you queues. Carry tissue packets anyway.

### Ordering kopi and teh

Start with the drink, then the milk, then the sugar and strength.

| Order | What you get |
|---|---|
| Kopi | Coffee with condensed milk |
| Kopi-c | Coffee with evaporated milk and sugar |
| Kopi-o | Black coffee with sugar |
| Kopi-o kosong | Black coffee, no sugar |
| Teh | Tea with condensed milk (the same modifiers apply) |
| Kosong | No sugar |
| Siu dai | Less sugar (some sources say less condensed milk) |
| Ga dai | More sugar |
| Po | Weaker |
| Gao | Stronger |
| Peng | Iced |
| Da bao or ta pau | Takeaway |

So "kopi-c siu dai peng" is iced coffee with evaporated milk and less sugar.

### Paying and timing

- Hawker stalls mostly take cash. Many display PayNow or SGQR codes, but those need a Singapore bank account. UPI works only at some NETS merchants and should not be relied on. Carry S$2, S$5 and S$10 notes and coins.
- Hawker centres, kopitiams and fast food chains add no service charge; the price shown is the price paid. Sit-down restaurants that show "++" add a 10 percent service charge and 9 percent GST, so multiply the menu price by 1.199. Tipping is not expected.
- The office lunch peak is about 12pm to 1:30pm on weekdays. Arrive by 11:30am or after 2pm.
- At seafood restaurants, crab is priced by weight, sometimes per 100 g rather than per kilo. Ask for the weight and the total before the crab is cooked; a 2023 dispute over a misread per-100 g price ended in a S$938 bill.
- Do not eat or drink on the MRT or buses, including water. Ask for "da bao" and eat after you get off.

## Dietary notes

- Pork is common at Chinese stalls: the char siu and roast meats, CMY's satay, Fu Shun at Maxwell, Hawker Chan and the bak kwa.
- Halal: the notes confirm halal status for Chef's Wok at NUS Frontier and describe Hjh Maimunah as a halal nasi padang. Look for the halal certificate at a stall. Not halal-certified: Ah Tai, Old Nyonya, Lina Confectionery and the 7-Eleven hot food bar. Birds of Paradise uses no alcohol or gelatine but is not halal-certified.
- Vegetarian options near Hillview: Green on Earth Vegetarian Cafe at The Rail Mall (daily 11am to 10pm), Rangooli (Indian) at The Rail Mall, Tandoori Culture (North Indian) at HillV2, a vegetarian stall at Senja Hawker Centre, and Living Wholesome Vegetarian Food at the Bukit Timah interim centre. Aunty Oats Pancake's batter contains no eggs. Many hawker sauces commonly include shrimp paste or fish sauce, so ask if that matters.
- Shellfish: Maxwell's cockles are raw or lightly blanched; skip them if either of you is wary. The oyster cake contains oysters, prawns and minced meat.
- Durian is banned on the MRT.
- Taking food home: pork products face import rules in India. If you buy bak kwa, buy sealed packs and declare them at Mumbai customs.
- Tap water is safe to drink; refill bottles at hawker centres and the airport, and drink before you board a train or bus.
