# Safety and practical tips

Singapore is very safe for visitors. The risks that matter on this trip are scam messages on your phone, a few strict rules that visitors break by accident, heat, and haze. This file covers those, plus entry, etiquette, emergency contacts, connectivity, phrases, power, and the iPhone purchase.

Prices are in S$ with INR at S$1 = ₹74.10 unless a section says otherwise.

## Safety and scams

- The main 2026 risks are digital. In February 2026 the Singapore Police Force warned about fake LTA text messages demanding unpaid tolls (at least 10 cases and S$24,000 lost in two weeks) and about pop-ups pretending a computer has been locked by the police. Genuine government text messages come only from the sender ID "gov.sg", and LTA messages never contain payment links. The police cannot lock or freeze your device.
- Treat any message about parcels, tolls, fines, SG Arrival Card "fees" or visa problems that contains a link as fake.
- Install ScamShield (iOS and Android). It blocks scam calls and flags scam messages. The 24-hour ScamShield helpline is 1799.
- Buy attraction tickets from the official site, the counter, or an established platform such as Klook or Pelago. Avoid resellers on Facebook, Telegram or Carousell. For the LANY concert, the venue says resold tickets will not be honoured.
- At seafood restaurants, crab is priced by weight, sometimes per 100 g rather than per kilo. Ask for the weight and total before it is cooked. A misread per-100 g price produced a S$938 bill in 2023.
- If you look at electronics, avoid the independent shops at Sim Lim Square. In 2014 a tourist there paid for an iPhone and was then pressed for S$1,500 in "warranty" fees; the shop owner and four others were arrested. The documented cases are from 2014 and 2015, but there is no reason to take the risk when the Apple stores are on your route.
- No official pickpocketing data was found. Take normal care in crowds: Chinatown, the MRT at peak hours, and Little India around Deepavali.

Customs on arrival:

- Alcohol: the duty-free concession needs at least 48 hours outside Singapore and does not apply when arriving from Malaysia. Sources disagree on the amounts (one combination is 1 litre of spirits plus 1 litre of wine, another is up to 2 litres in total). Bring at most one or two bottles bought after leaving India, or none.
- Medicines: carry prescription medicines in their original packaging with a doctor's letter. Codeine cough syrups common in India are a known risk; check the HSA rules on personal medication before you pack anything with codeine or strong painkillers.

## Weather, haze and health

### What to expect

- Early November is the second inter-monsoon period. The Northeast Monsoon starts in December. Expect hot, humid, bright mornings and afternoon or early-evening thunderstorms, sometimes heavy but usually short.
- Long-run November averages (1991 to 2020, Changi): 252.3 mm of rain over about 19 rain days, daily highs of 31.2°C, lows of 24.6°C, humidity near 85 percent, and 22 lightning days, the most of any month. Winds are the lightest of the year, which makes the humidity feel heavier and lets haze linger.
- This year is different from the average. MSS reports strong El Niño conditions, expected to peak from October to December 2026, and a positive Indian Ocean Dipole through November. Both point to drier and hotter weather than usual. Early October highs were forecast at 34°C to 35°C.
- The early Open-Meteo forecast fetched on 25 October shows highs of 30°C to 31°C and feels-like temperatures of 31°C to 37°C for 2 to 9 November, with UV around 8 to 9. It is low confidence at that range. The day-by-day figures are in `itinerary.md`. Check the MSS fortnightly outlook, due around 31 October or 1 November.
- Plan outdoor activity before 11am and after 5pm. Mornings are the reliable dry window.

### Haze

- 2026 is a haze year. Smoke from fires in Sumatra and Kalimantan pushed the 24-hour PSI into the unhealthy range from September. Central Singapore reached 181 to 191 on 9 October, and a 16 October report cited 225. An NEA note around 15 October expected a wind shift that could reduce the haze. No later readings were found.
- PSI bands: 0 to 50 good, 51 to 100 moderate, 101 to 200 unhealthy, 201 to 300 very unhealthy, above 300 hazardous. At 101 to 200, healthy adults should cut down prolonged or strenuous outdoor exertion; people with heart or lung conditions should avoid it.
- Check the NEA haze page or the myENV app each morning from 30 October. If the PSI is high, swap the outdoor-heavy parts of a day (the Southern Ridges, East Coast Park cycling, Sungei Buloh) for indoor stops: the National Gallery, the malls, the Gardens' domes, Jewel.
- Bring about 10 N95 masks from India; they are cheaper and certain. Guardian and Watsons sell them during haze periods.

### Other health points

- Dengue: cases are low by recent standards (79 in the week to 10 October), but three red-alert clusters were active in mid-October: Lentor Gardens, Bishan Street 12 and the Ho Ching Road area. Use repellent with DEET or picaridin at Sungei Buloh, the Zoo, the trails near Hillview and the kampong.
- Heat: carry at least a litre of water each for an outdoor half-day, and refill at hawker centres. Tap water is safe to drink. Drink before you board a train or bus.
- Lightning: outdoor facilities may suspend operations during lightning alerts. Leave open paths and bridges when thunder starts.
- Macaques live in the parks near Hillview. Do not feed them or carry food openly.
- Healthcare for foreigners is charged at full, unsubsidised rates, so carry travel insurance with good medical cover (about ₹1,000 each for eight days under 40 is the budget notes' estimate; at least US$50,000 to US$100,000 of medical cover with cashless hospital tie-ups in Singapore is the suggestion). Check that cycling and trail walking are covered.

### Packing list

- About 10 N95 masks.
- Two Type G plug adapters.
- Refillable water bottles.
- SPF 50 sunscreen, hats and sunglasses.
- Mosquito repellent.
- A compact umbrella or packable rain jacket.
- Light cotton or linen clothing.
- One long-sleeved layer or shawl each, for cold air-conditioning in malls, cinemas and taxis and for temples.
- Ankle-length bottoms and sleeved tops for the Sultan Mosque (robes are also lent).
- Slip-on shoes for temples, and closed shoes with grip for the Southern Ridges and Rail Corridor.
- Oral rehydration salts.
- Prescription medicines with a doctor's letter.
- Printed copies of the e-visa, the SG Arrival Card confirmation, the accommodation booking and the insurance policy.
- Tissue packets for hawker centres.
- Leave behind: vapes and e-cigarettes, cigarettes, chewing gum and codeine cough syrups.

## Entry

- Visas: both of you already hold visas. A visa does not guarantee entry; ICA officers grant the visit pass at the checkpoint.
- Passport validity: widely reported guidance is at least six months from the date of arrival. Confirm on ICA's entry requirements page.
- SG Arrival Card: each of you must submit one within the three days before arrival. For a 2 November arrival, the window opens on Saturday 31 October, Singapore time. It is free, through the ICA SG Arrival Card e-Service or the MyICA app only. Use an email address you check on your phone, because the e-Pass is sent there. Ignore any third-party site that charges a fee.
- Automated clearance: all travellers with an ICAO-compliant passport can use the automated lanes on arrival with no prior enrolment. Do not cover your face at the lanes.

## Etiquette at temples, the mosque and the kampong

### Temples

- Buddha Tooth Relic Temple (Day 2): shoulders and knees covered; shorts, mini-skirts and off-shoulder tops are not allowed; sarongs are lent at the entrance. No photography in the fourth-floor relic chamber. Official hours 9am to 5pm. Free; donations welcome.
- Sri Mariamman Temple (Day 2): remove shoes at the entrance and cover shoulders and knees. Its own hours and rules were not verified. It will be very crowded on Deepavali, Sunday 8 November, so your Tuesday visit avoids that.
- Thian Hock Keng (Day 6): modest dress. Hours listed as 7:30am to 5pm by one guide.

### Sultan Mosque (Day 7)

- Visitor hours are commonly listed as Saturday to Thursday 10am to 12pm and 2pm to 4pm, and Friday 2:30pm to 4pm, with closures for prayers at 12:30pm to 2pm and 4pm to 5pm. Sources disagree, so check on the day. The plan has you there from about 2pm.
- Men: sleeved tops and long trousers. Women: long sleeves and ankle-length bottoms. Free robes are lent at the counter. Shoes off.
- Visitors may not enter the prayer hall unless accompanied by a mosque official.

### Kampong Lorong Buangkok (Day 7)

- The kampong is private land with about 30 households. A 2012 report said villagers had charged entrance fees to sightseers, and a 2017 report described residents as wary of visitors.
- Go in daylight. Keep to the shared lane, keep voices low and do not enter yards.
- Photograph exteriors only, never inside homes, and ask before photographing a person. Do not fly a drone.
- Greet residents if they catch your eye.

### General

- Tipping is not expected. Restaurants that show "++" already add a 10 percent service charge and 9 percent GST.
- Queues are orderly. On escalators, stand on the left and walk on the right. Let passengers off the train before you board.
- The local name for the festival is Deepavali rather than Diwali; "Happy Deepavali" is the greeting.

## Plugs, voltage and payments

- Singapore uses Type G plugs (three rectangular pins) at 230 V, 50 Hz. This is standard but was not sourced in the notes. Indian Type C and D plugs do not fit, so bring adapters; the voltage matches India, so no converter is needed.
- Payments: use a zero-markup card for transport, chains and restaurants, and carry cash for hawker stalls. Withdraw from bank ATMs (DBS, OCBC, UOB) and always choose to be charged in SGD, not INR. There is an ATM in the Hillview MRT concourse. UPI works only at some NETS merchants, so do not rely on it. More detail is in `budget_summary.md`.

## The iPhone purchase

Source: the `iphone_purchase.md` research note, dated 25 October 2026. It uses S$1 = ₹74.29, so its rupee figures differ slightly from the rest of this guide.

- Where and when: Apple Marina Bay Sands on Tuesday 3 November (10am to 10pm in late October; November hours were not yet published), with Apple Orchard Road on Thursday 5 November as the backup. A walk-in purchase is the only practical route: the Apple Singapore online store accepts only Singapore billing addresses, and pickup orders appear not to qualify for the tourist refund.
- At the store: the buyer brings the original passport, not a copy, and a card in the same name. Before paying, ask the Specialist to confirm that an eTRS (tourist refund) transaction will be issued; the notes found no 2025 or 2026 report confirming this for Apple's stores. Pay in SGD and decline any offer to charge in rupees. Check that the receipt shows the serial number and the eTRS ticket.
- Keep the box sealed. Goods must not be used in Singapore, and an opened product can be refused.
- GST refund at Changi: GST is 9 percent, so a S$1,899 iPhone 18 Pro (256 GB) contains S$156.80 of GST. After the handling fee, the realistic net refund is about S$133 to S$142 (₹9,875 to ₹10,580 at the note's rate). Because the phone travels in hand luggage, claim at an eTRS kiosk in the Terminal 3 transit area after immigration. If flagged, take the sealed phone, receipt, passport and boarding pass to the Customs Inspection Counter. Choose a refund to the credit card (credited within 10 days). Allow 20 to 30 minutes. The counters run 24 hours, so the holiday does not matter. The same person must buy, carry and claim.
- Indian customs: the duty-free allowance under the Baggage Rules, 2026 (in force since 2 February 2026) is ₹75,000 per person, and the two of you cannot pool your allowances. A new sealed phone counts against it. Duty on the value above the allowance is about 35 percent according to the most specific source; one source says 38.5 percent. Declare the phone in the red channel at Mumbai; you can pre-file the declaration on the ATITHI app. Using the green channel with dutiable goods risks confiscation, a penalty and a fine, with duty still payable.
- Is it worth it: the note's worked example, a 256 GB iPhone 18 Pro bought for S$1,899, refunded and declared, lands about ₹11,000 cheaper than the Apple India price of ₹1,64,900, with a zero-markup card and duty at 35 percent. At 38.5 percent the saving is about ₹8,600, and with a card that charges a 3.5 percent markup it is about ₹5,100. It is roughly break-even at 1 TB and above on the Pro, and the larger Pro Max models save little or cost more. If the person carrying the phone also brings back other new goods that use up their allowance, the whole phone becomes dutiable and it costs more than buying in India, so that person should keep other shopping light.
- Compatibility: Singapore and India sell the same iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max models (A3714 and A3717), with a nano-SIM tray plus eSIM, so bands match an Indian phone. Avoid US, Japanese and Gulf units.
- Warranty: Apple may restrict warranty service to the country of sale, and reports on Apple India honouring Singapore-bought iPhones are mixed and mostly old, so warranty in India is not assured. Set the phone up and test it as soon as you are home. The note advises against buying Singapore AppleCare+ for a phone that will live in India.
- Card limits: raise the buyer's international and per-transaction limit to cover at least S$2,200 before you leave, and tell the bank about the trip.
