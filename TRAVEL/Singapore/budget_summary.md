# Budget summary

All figures are for the two of you unless marked "each" or "per person". Prices are in Singapore dollars, converted at S$1 = ₹74.10, the mid-market rate on 22 October 2026 (Currency.wiki) used in the budget notes. Banks and forex cards give a slightly worse rate, so add a 1 to 2 percent buffer to the rupee figures. INR totals use Indian digit grouping (₹1,23,747 is ₹1.24 lakh).

Accommodation is already paid and is excluded from the spending totals. It appears only as a reference line. The iPhone is also excluded; it has its own section below.

Most figures in the tables are estimates built from cited unit prices (fares, tickets, dish prices). Where no source price exists, the budget notes used judgement, and those items are labelled.

## The iPhone

Source: the `iphone_purchase.md` research note (25 October 2026). It uses S$1 = ₹74.29, so its rupee figures are slightly higher than this file's. The purchase is not in any total above.

- Price at Apple Marina Bay Sands: the iPhone 18 Pro 256 GB is S$1,899 including GST (₹1,41,069 at the note's rate). The Apple India price is ₹1,64,900.
- GST refund: none. You will use the phone during the trip, and the refund only covers goods not used in Singapore.
- Indian customs: the allowance is ₹75,000 per person under the Baggage Rules, 2026, and cannot be pooled. Duty is about 35 percent on the value above it (one source says 38.5 percent). Declare the phone in the red channel at Mumbai.
- Worked example: one 256 GB Pro, paid with a zero-markup card and declared: price ₹1,41,069, duty about ₹23,124 on the ₹66,069 above the allowance, landed cost about ₹1,64,193. That is about ₹700 below the Apple India price of ₹1,64,900, so roughly level.

If the person carrying the phone also brings back other new goods that use up their ₹75,000, the whole phone becomes dutiable and it costs more than buying in India.

- Card: pay in SGD with a zero-markup credit card in the buyer's name, decline any offer to charge in rupees, and raise the card's international limit to at least S$2,200 before you leave. Card spend abroad is not subject to TCS on current reporting.

## Payment and currency

- Cards: each of you should carry one zero-markup Visa or Mastercard and tap it consistently for transit, plus a backup card on a different network. Niyo Global (SBM Bank, prepaid Visa) and Scapia (Federal Bank, credit card) are named as zero-markup; some cards sold as zero-markup still charge 1.5 to 3.5 percent, so check. On the expected spend, a 3.5 percent markup would cost about S$58 (₹4,298).
- Cash: take about S$200 in cash (₹14,820), from India or a money changer. Cash covers hawker stalls (assume S$60 to S$90 a day for two in cash unless you see a card or QR sign), the S$50 (₹3,705) cash deposit for GoCycling bikes, the Pulau Ubin bumboat, and shops such as Mei Heong Yuen that take only cash or PayNow. Mustafa Centre (open 24 hours) usually gives the best INR rate; The Arcade at Raffles Place has many changers side by side. Mustafa is in Little India, which you ruled out, so The Arcade, near Telok Ayer on Day 2 or Day 6, is the convenient choice.
- ATMs: use bank ATMs (DBS, OCBC, UOB). There is one in the Hillview MRT concourse. Always choose to be charged in SGD, not INR; dynamic currency conversion adds roughly 2 to 7 percent according to Indian bank guides.
- UPI: the UPI to PayNow link is mainly for person-to-person transfers, and merchant acceptance is limited to some NETS merchants. Enable international UPI and test it with a small payment if you like, but treat any success as a bonus.
- PayNow and SGQR codes at stalls need a Singapore bank account.
- Restaurant bills with "++" are multiplied by 1.199 (10 percent service charge plus 9 percent GST on the total). Hawker centres, kopitiams and fast food chains add nothing.
- GST refund on other shopping: at least S$100 including GST, with up to three same-day invoices from the same retailer combinable. The goods must leave in your luggage within two months of purchase.

## Where to save and where to spend

Where to save (estimated saving for two):

- Check Klook and KKday for the Gardens by the Bay domes the week before: up to about S$34 (₹2,519), if the affiliate blog's S$29 price holds. Buy on the official site if the gap has closed.
- Compare a Grab quote with the S$70 (₹5,187) Concierge fare on arrival, and book the 04:30 departure on Grab instead if it quotes below the metered estimate: S$10 to S$20 (₹741 to ₹1,482).
- Eat breakfast near Hillview (Ya Kun, Toast Box, Springleaf Prata Place) instead of in tourist centres: S$5 to S$10 (₹371 to ₹741) a meal.
- Start the East Coast Park bikes before 5pm on the Friday and ask about the free second hour: about S$20 (₹1,482).
- Pay as you go on the MRT rather than buying a Tourist Pass, and tap in before 7:45am on weekday mornings for 50 cents off each fare.
- Skip the Night Safari unless it matters to you: it adds about S$160 (₹11,856) with dinner and the late ride.
- Use a zero-markup card: about S$58 (₹4,298) against a 3.5 percent card.
- Bring N95 masks and refillable bottles from India: S$10 to S$30 (₹741 to ₹2,223).
- Do not prebook the Orchid Garden; Klook charges the same as the gate.

Where to spend:

- The crab dinner at Keng Eng Kee. It is the one planned splurge, and choosing a reputable place matters more than saving S$50.
- The Zoo. S$98 (₹7,262) for two is fair for a full day.
- The taxi to Sungei Buloh for the 7am start, and the Grab between Sungei Buloh and the Zoo if the buses are slow. Each saves about an hour in the heat.
- The Gardens by the Bay domes and the National Gallery, which are central to the plan and have no cheaper substitute.
- Late rides home. A S$20 to S$25 (₹1,482 to ₹1,853) Grab after a long evening is better value than a long MRT ride at closing time, and much cheaper than a taxi after midnight.
- Travel insurance at about ₹1,000 each.
